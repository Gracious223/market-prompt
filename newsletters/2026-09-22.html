Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your analysts and portfolio managers use daily — and what they mean for the AI products you build for them.

Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net income divided by shares outstanding. The single most cited metric in equity analysis. AI tools that track, forecast, and contextualise EPS — especially at earnings season — are among the highest-frequency use cases on the buy side.
Short Selling
Borrowing a security and selling it, hoping to buy it back cheaper later. Short sellers express negative views on a stock. AI-generated short thesis research is one of the fastest-growing use cases — and one of the most reputationally sensitive.
Yield Spread
The difference in yield between two bonds — typically vs. a "risk-free" benchmark like US Treasuries or UK Gilts. Wider spreads signal more perceived risk. AI tools that monitor spread movements in real time are valuable early-warning signals for credit analysts.
Sector Rotation
Shifting capital from one sector to another as economic conditions change (e.g. moving from tech to energy as rates rise). AI pattern-recognition tools that detect early rotation signals are used by macro portfolio managers to rebalance ahead of the crowd.
Order Book
A live record of all buy and sell orders for a security at different price levels. The "depth" of an order book reveals liquidity conditions. AI tools that read and interpret order book data give traders real-time insight into market microstructure.
Benchmark Index
A standard reference portfolio used to evaluate performance — e.g. S&P 500, FTSE 100, MSCI World. Portfolio managers are judged against their benchmark. AI products must surface whether a signal is benchmark-relative or absolute — that framing changes how analysts interpret it.

Workflow Reality Check · September 2026

Finance Teams Are Speeding Up Tasks With AI — But Failing to Fix the Cash Cycle

A new September 2026 report (GlobeNewsWire) finds AI is accelerating task completion in finance functions — but cash cycle times remain stubbornly unchanged. Firms are optimising at the task level while the end-to-end process remains unimproved. The productivity gain isn't translating into outcome improvement.

Why It Matters

A critical product design warning: automating a single step in a broken workflow doesn't fix the workflow. If your AI tool speeds up research but analysts still can't act on it faster, the product hasn't created value. Map the end-to-end flow, not just the task.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's edition focuses on risk and measurement vocabulary — the language analysts use when assessing portfolio exposure, fixed income dynamics, and diversification. These terms appear in the Apollo and BCG stories above and will come up constantly in analyst conversations.

Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a given period. Maximum drawdown is a core risk metric — AI risk tools that flag drawdown thresholds in real time are among the most requested buy-side analytics features.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes — measured in years. A bond with 5-year duration falls ~5% in price if rates rise 1%. Fixed income analysts watch duration closely; AI tools that recalculate duration across a portfolio instantly are high-value.
Basis Points (bps)
One basis point equals 0.01% — the standard unit for interest rates, spreads, and yield changes. "Rates rose 25bps" means rates moved up 0.25%. You will hear this constantly from fixed income analysts and portfolio managers.
Spread
The difference between two yields or prices. A credit spread is the yield premium a corporate bond pays over a risk-free government bond. Wider spreads signal higher perceived risk. Spread monitoring is a core use case for real-time AI analytics in credit markets.
Correlation
A statistical measure (−1 to +1) of how two assets move together. Diversification depends on low correlation — assets that move independently reduce portfolio risk. Higher equity-bond correlation in recent years has challenged the traditional 60/40 portfolio model.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of assets a fund or firm manages on behalf of clients. AUM is the primary measure of a fund's scale. A product serving a $500B AUM firm has very different scalability and compliance requirements than one serving a $2B boutique.

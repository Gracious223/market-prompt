Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms you will hear in meetings with analysts and portfolio managers. Understanding these precisely lets you ask better questions, write sharper PRDs, and earn credibility in rooms where financial fluency is assumed.

Short Selling
Borrowing shares, selling them, and hoping to buy them back cheaper later. Short sellers profit when prices fall. AI tools that surface short interest data or unusual short activity give analysts an early signal on market sentiment.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
The per-unit value of a fund: total assets minus liabilities, divided by units outstanding. NAV is calculated daily for mutual funds and ETFs. Analysts tracking fund flows use NAV movements as a signal of institutional positioning.
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between what buyers will pay (bid) and sellers will accept (ask). A narrow spread signals high liquidity; a wide spread signals illiquidity or uncertainty. In AI-driven markets, spreads can compress and widen in milliseconds.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net income divided by shares outstanding. The most-quoted single number in equity research. AI tools that extract, track, and compare EPS across companies and time periods are among the highest-demand analyst applications.
Gilt
A UK government bond — the British equivalent of a US Treasury. When gilt yields rise, borrowing costs for UK companies rise too. Your users hold gilts in fixed-income portfolios and watch gilt markets as a proxy for UK rate expectations.
Custodian
An institution (usually a bank) that holds and safeguards financial assets on behalf of clients. BNY Mellon and State Street are examples. Custodians are critical infrastructure — and increasingly a data source for AI products serving institutional investors.

FCA · UK

FCA Sets Out Next Phase of Smarter, More Effective Regulation

The FCA has published its next phase of regulatory evolution, doubling down on principles-based oversight rather than AI-specific rules. The regulator will intervene only in "egregious failures," citing AI's evolution every three to six months as the reason prescriptive rules would always lag. Consumer Duty and SM&CR remain the operative frameworks for AI outputs.

Why It Matters

Principles-based regulation means you are accountable for defining what "good outcomes" look like for your users — the FCA will judge your product against its effects, not against a checklist. This is harder, not easier, than rules-based compliance.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's Consumer Duty guidance — write down three ways your current product could fail the "good outcomes" test
  • Under SM&CR: who in your firm is the Senior Manager accountable if an AI output leads to client harm?
  • Research "principles-based vs. rules-based regulation" — which approach do other jurisdictions (EU, US) favour, and how does that affect cross-border product design?

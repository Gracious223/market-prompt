Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily — building the vocabulary that lets you ask sharper product questions and earn the room.

Yield Curve
A graph plotting interest rates against bond maturities, from short to long term. A "normal" curve slopes up (longer bonds yield more). An "inverted" curve — short rates above long — has historically preceded recessions and is one of the most-watched macro signals.
Beta
How much a stock moves relative to the market. A beta of 1.2 means the stock tends to move 20% more than the market in either direction. High-beta stocks amplify gains and losses. PMs use beta to tune a portfolio's overall market sensitivity.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio or strategy. A 20% drawdown means the fund fell 20% from its highest point before recovering. AI-driven strategies are often evaluated partly on their maximum drawdown — how bad did it get, and how quickly did it recover?
Basis Points (bps)
The standard unit for interest rate and spread changes. 1 basis point = 0.01%. A 25bps rate cut means rates fell by 0.25%. When a PM talks about a "50bps spread widening," they mean corporate bond yields rose 0.50% relative to government bonds.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell them now, hoping to buy them back cheaper later. Shorts profit when prices fall. Hedge funds use shorts to hedge long positions or express a bearish view. AI tools that flag deteriorating fundamentals early are particularly valuable for short-side research.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
The value of a fund's assets minus its liabilities, usually calculated daily. For mutual funds and ETFs, NAV is the per-share value of what the fund actually holds. AI tools that help PMs track real-time NAV movement across positions are a core use case in your space.

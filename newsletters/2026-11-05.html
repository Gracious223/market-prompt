Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's stories circle a single question: will the AI investment cycle generate real returns? These six terms give you the vocabulary to hold your own in any conversation about Anthropic's IPO, the AI capex debate, or what Goldman's Solomon actually means when he talks about applications beating infrastructure.

IPO (Initial Public Offering)
When a private company sells shares to the public for the first time. Anthropic is preparing to list as ANT on Nasdaq — the roadshow is the marketing tour to institutional investors before shares are priced and trading begins.
CapEx (Capital Expenditure)
Spending on physical or long-lived assets — in AI, this means data centres, chips, and infrastructure. Morgan Stanley puts AI CapEx at $10–15T over the next decade. The debate is whether this spending converts into earnings fast enough.
Sector Rotation
When investors move capital from one market sector into another. Right now, some investors are rotating out of AI infrastructure stocks into sectors that benefit from AI applications — the layer Solomon says will capture long-term value.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
Share price divided by earnings per share. A high P/E means investors are paying a premium for expected future growth. AI companies with no current earnings trade on narrative P/E — which is why investors are now demanding proof of returns.
Prospectus (S-1)
The document a company files with the SEC before going public. It discloses financials, risks, and business model. Anthropic's S-1 is public — its risk factors tell you exactly what a regulated enterprise AI buyer should be worried about.
Free Cash Flow (FCF)
Cash generated by the business after capital expenditure. It's the clearest signal that a company is profitable in practice. Investors now demanding AI "pay off" are essentially demanding positive FCF — not just revenue growth or capability claims.

FCA Issues First Practical AI Guidance — Good and Poor Practice Named for UK Firms

After sustained pressure from the Treasury Committee, the FCA published practical guidance on October 30 showing how existing rules apply to AI — with named examples of good and poor practice. The FCA expects Senior Managers to own AI risks explicitly, and requires continuous monitoring of AI outputs. Firms remain liable for AI services even when outsourced.

Why It Matters

This is the clearest signal yet about what UK regulators consider acceptable AI governance. The "good practice" examples are effectively a product checklist — explainability, auditability, and SM&CR-mapped accountability are now the minimum bar.

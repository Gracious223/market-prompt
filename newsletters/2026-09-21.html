Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms from the vocabulary your users speak fluently. Understanding these precisely means asking sharper questions, writing PRDs that resonate with analysts, and holding your own in rooms where financial fluency is assumed.

Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01% (one hundredth of one percent). All yield spreads, rate moves, and fee differences in finance are quoted in bps. If your product surfaces rate or spread data, it must use this unit — "25bps" not "0.25%."
Duration Risk
In fixed income, the sensitivity of a bond's price to interest rate changes. A longer-duration bond falls more when rates rise. AI tools that model interest rate scenarios help portfolio managers stress-test duration exposure across large bond portfolios.
P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
A company's share price divided by its annual earnings per share. The most quoted equity valuation metric. AI tools that track, benchmark, and compare P/E ratios across sectors and time periods are fundamental to how equity analysts work.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a given period. A "max drawdown" of 15% means the portfolio fell 15% from its highest point before recovering. Risk-aware AI outputs should always be framed in terms of drawdown, not just return.
Liquidity Risk
The risk of being unable to sell an asset quickly at a fair price. Illiquid positions are dangerous in volatile markets. AI products that flag liquidity risk — especially across less-traded fixed income instruments — offer direct value to portfolio managers managing large positions.
Market Capitalisation
Total market value of a company: share price × shares outstanding. Splits into large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap. AI screening tools commonly allow analysts to filter universes by market cap — understanding these tiers is essential for scoping your product's coverage decisions.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily. Building this vocabulary lets you ask sharper questions, write better PRDs, and earn credibility in investment conversations.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × total shares outstanding. It classifies companies as large-cap, mid-cap, or small-cap. AI tools often filter and screen securities by market cap as a first-pass risk proxy.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
Share price ÷ earnings per share. A core valuation metric. A high P/E implies growth expectations; a low one implies value or risk. LLMs are being used to contextualise P/E moves against narrative signals.
Short Selling
Borrowing and selling a security in expectation of buying it back cheaper. Short sellers are often the first to identify corporate fraud — AI-assisted short research is a growing use case at hedge funds.
Derivatives
Financial instruments whose value derives from an underlying asset — options, futures, swaps. Used to hedge risk or take leveraged positions. Increasingly, AI agents are being tested on derivatives pricing desks.
Active vs. Passive Investing
Active: a manager picks securities to beat a benchmark. Passive: a fund tracks an index. The active/passive debate is shifting again as AI challenges the assumption that beating the market requires human judgment.
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between the price a buyer will pay (bid) and the price a seller will accept (ask). A wider spread signals lower liquidity. AI is compressing spreads in some markets by improving price discovery speed.

FCA · UK · Breaking

FCA Sets Out Formal Rules of the Road for Agentic AI in Financial Services

On 24 November, the FCA published its landmark "Our approach to Agentic AI" guidance — the first formal expectations document for autonomous AI agents operating in regulated financial services. The framework stays principles-based and outcome-focused: no new AI rulebook, but a clear signal that tech providers may need authorisation if their agents carry out regulated activities. Senior managers must take "reasonable steps" to understand AI risks. Firms remain responsible when third-party AI systems cause harm.

Why It Matters

This directly governs your product. If your AI agent executes, recommends, or influences a regulated activity, your firm — and named senior managers — carry accountability. This is not a future concern.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's "Our approach to Agentic AI" page directly — bookmark it as a standing reference
  • Under SM&CR: identify the Senior Manager at your firm who would be accountable for an AI-caused harm today
  • What does "authorisation" mean for an AI agent? Read Freshfields' analysis for a legal view

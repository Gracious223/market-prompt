Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six terms that come up constantly in the current AI-and-markets moment — from the BofA crowded-trade signal to the FCA's vulnerability-response question. Understand these before you design tools for the people who use them daily.

Beta
A stock's sensitivity to market-wide moves. Beta of 1 tracks the index. Above 1 amplifies it; below 1 cushions it. AI tools use beta to characterise and manage portfolio risk exposure in real time.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell now and buying back cheaper later — a bet on price decline. Elevated short interest is a signal AI can surface quickly. Heavily regulated in some markets; banned temporarily during crises.
Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01%. Rate moves, yield spreads, and fee changes are quoted in bps. "Spreads widened 50bps" means half a percentage point. Essential vocabulary for any fixed-income analytics tool.
Drawdown
Peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value. Maximum drawdown is a key risk metric. An AI tool that flags drawdown risk before it materialises — rather than measuring it after — is providing genuine analyst value.
Crowded Trade
When too many investors hold the same position. The current 37% tech overweight is a textbook crowded-trade signal. Crowded trades unwind fast and in unison — and AI that detects concentration risk is increasingly in demand.
Redemption
When a fund investor withdraws their capital. In a stress event, redemptions force managers to sell — accelerating losses. Understanding redemption dynamics is critical context for any liquidity-monitoring or stress-scenario AI tool.

Cambridge Judge: Autonomous Investment Agents Could Amplify Market Crashes

Cambridge Judge Business School's analysis of the agentic AI era in financial services warns that autonomous investment agents, by lowering the barrier to entry, may react to the same signals simultaneously — amplifying moves that a human-paced market would absorb. Governance frameworks are not yet ready for agents that execute, not just advise.

Why It Matters

If your agents execute or inform fast-moving decisions, their aggregate behaviour in a stressed market is a risk your product specification must address. The "human-in-the-loop" design is not optional — it is your regulatory and systemic risk control.

BoE · Payments

BoE and FCA Launch Joint Work on Agentic AI in Payments and Financial Markets

The Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee has formally asked the BoE and FCA to undertake focused work on agentic AI use cases in payments and financial markets — a first formal regulatory acknowledgment that agentic systems operating in settlement and execution contexts require dedicated oversight, separate from existing AI governance frameworks.

Why It Matters

Payments and execution are the two domains where agentic AI moves fastest and causes most harm when it fails. Regulatory attention here will define the acceptable design space for autonomous financial agents — and that shapes your product's permissible scope.

Research & Think About

  • Research what "T+1 settlement" means and how agentic AI is already being used to compress settlement cycles
  • Consider: if your product ever touches execution or payments, what does the BoE's new focus mean for your governance model?

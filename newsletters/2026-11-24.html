70% of Buy-Side Firms Are Using AI — But Only 5% Have Truly Integrated It

SimCorp's 2026 InvestOps Report found 70% of buy-side firms using AI in the front office, up from roughly 10% a year earlier. But Acuity Analytics found only 5% describe it as "fully integrated" — concentrated almost entirely among managers above $100 billion AUM. The adoption curve is steep; meaningful depth is not.

Why It Matters

The 65-percentage-point gap between adoption and integration is your product opportunity. What stops mid-size managers from reaching full integration? That's your discovery backlog, not a background statistic.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more building blocks of the language your users speak every day — each one shapes decisions your product will ultimately inform.

P/E Ratio
Price-to-earnings: a stock's price divided by its annual earnings per share. The most common valuation shorthand. A high P/E signals investors expect strong growth; a low one may indicate undervaluation or declining prospects.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell them now, hoping to buy them back cheaper later. Profit if the price falls; loss if it rises. AI tools are increasingly used to identify short-selling signals from alternative data.
Volatility / VIX
Volatility measures how much an asset's price swings. The VIX is Wall Street's "fear index" — a real-time measure of expected S&P 500 volatility. High VIX = market uncertainty. AI-driven trading can amplify volatility.
Tracking Error
How closely a fund's returns match its benchmark. A low tracking error means the fund is behaving like its index. Active managers tolerate higher tracking error in pursuit of alpha. AI tools help monitor and explain divergence.
Market Capitalisation
Total market value of a company's outstanding shares (share price × shares outstanding). Used to classify companies: large-cap (£10B+), mid-cap, small-cap. Affects liquidity, analyst coverage, and index inclusion.
Yield Curve
A chart of bond yields across different maturities. Normally slopes upward (longer = higher yield). An inverted yield curve — short-term rates above long-term — has historically preceded recessions and is closely watched by PMs.

Research · Capgemini

Finance Is Moving From Automation to Autonomy — Explainability Is the Last Wall

Capgemini's report on agentic AI in financial services tracks the shift from rule-based automation to multi-step autonomous decision-making. The barrier to full deployment isn't capability — it's explainability. Regulators and risk functions need to know why a decision was made, not just what it was.

Why It Matters

Your product will face the same wall. Building explainability into your agentic workflows from day one is cheaper than retrofitting it after a regulator asks. This is a product architecture decision, not a legal one.

FCA Launches World-First AI Spotlight Observatory — 200+ Firms Catalogued Live

The FCA launched the AI Spotlight Observatory on 19 November 2026 — described as the first resource of its kind globally. Built with CFTE and technology partner NayaOne, it draws on real-world AI use cases from 200+ firms across financial crime, agentic payments, compliance, and consumer finance. FCA · Consumer Finance

FCA Names AI Good Practice in Consumer Finance — and the Gaps Are Just as Clear

The FCA's November 2026 consumer finance AI findings define good practice for the first time — and explicitly name areas where firms are falling short. The Consumer Duty lens applies: firms must show AI outputs produce fair outcomes, not just faster ones. Explainability, auditability, and human oversight top the good practice list.

Why It Matters

"Fair outcome" is not an engineering metric — it's a product decision about what your AI optimises for. The FCA has now published what good looks like. If your product can't map to it, that's a risk sitting in your PRD today.

Skills · Evaluation Design

Evaluation Design Now Belongs in the Product Spec — Not the Engineering Spec

A Roadmaps Conference 2026 piece argues that evaluation sets, human review sampling, and a tolerated-error budget now belong in the product spec — written by the PM, not handed off to engineers. PMs who can define what "correct" looks like for each AI output type are increasingly rare and command a premium in hiring.

Why It Matters

If you can't specify how your AI product should be tested, you can't hold your engineers accountable for quality — and your analysts can't trust the outputs. Evals are your quality framework, and they start with product, not engineering.

Research & Think About
  • Read the Roadmaps piece: roadmapsconference.com/blog/ai-product-management-2026 — specifically the section on evaluation design
  • Define one eval for your current AI product: pick one output type and write what "correct" looks like — not vaguely, specifically
  • Research "LLM-as-a-judge" and "tolerated-error budget" — both are now standard PM vocabulary in AI-native teams

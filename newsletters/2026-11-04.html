Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's stories hinge on concepts from quantitative investing and market structure. These six terms give you the vocabulary to hold your own in conversations about what happened in quant funds this week — and what it means for the tools you build.

Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a fund's value. A 25% drawdown means a fund lost a quarter of its value from its recent high. Today's quant funds are experiencing this after AI-crowded trades reversed.
Systematic vs. Discretionary
Systematic funds use rules-based, algorithmic models to make decisions. Discretionary funds rely on human judgement. Most quant hedge funds are systematic — which is why AI-driven herding can hit them all at once.
Crowded Trade
When many market participants hold the same position based on similar signals. Reversals become violent because everyone exits simultaneously. AI signal convergence is accelerating crowding risk across quant funds.
Market Capitalisation
The total market value of a company's outstanding shares (share price × shares outstanding). "Large-cap", "mid-cap" and "small-cap" refer to this. Anthropic's $2T IPO target would make it one of the world's largest companies.
Credit Spread
The yield difference between a corporate bond and a risk-free government bond of the same maturity. Wider spreads signal perceived credit risk. AI tools that monitor spreads in real time are among the most compelling fixed-income applications.
Momentum
An investment factor: assets that have risen recently tend to keep rising in the short term. Quant funds heavily exploit momentum signals, which is why AI-driven momentum trades can become dangerously correlated across the market.

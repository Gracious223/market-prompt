Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily. Understanding these helps you build better products and earn credibility in the room.

Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01%. Fee changes, yield spreads and performance attribution are all quoted in bps. When a PM says "we're charging 50bps," that's 0.5%. Critical vocabulary in every product conversation.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
Total value of a fund's assets minus its liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. The price at which investors buy or redeem fund units. AI tools that affect fund valuation directly touch NAV.
Drawdown
Peak-to-trough decline of a portfolio over a period. A 20% drawdown means the fund fell 20% from its high. Max drawdown is a key risk metric. Today's Forbes story centres on AI-correlated drawdowns.
Liquidity Risk
The risk that an asset cannot be sold quickly enough at a fair price. AI-driven correlated selling can turn normally liquid assets illiquid suddenly — a core systemic risk concern.
Benchmark
The index against which a fund's performance is measured (e.g. FTSE 100, S&P 500). Every active portfolio manager is judged against a benchmark. Alpha is what they earn above it. Your AI tools should speak this language.
Stress Testing
Simulating extreme market scenarios to see how a portfolio holds up. Required by regulators. AI is now used to generate and run stress scenarios at speed humans cannot match. If your product touches risk, understand the output.

FCA: Tech Branding Won't Exempt AI Agent Builders From Authorisation

On 24 November, the FCA issued agentic AI expectations: calling yourself a technology provider does not remove the need for FCA authorisation when agents initiate payments, arrange credit or make financial promotions. Regulated firms remain fully responsible for any third-party AI agents they deploy. No new rules — Consumer Duty and SM&CR apply now.

Why It Matters

If your product's AI agents touch any regulated activity — directly or indirectly — your firm needs authorisation clarity before launch, not after.

PRA · FCA · BoE

UK Regulators Issue First Joint AI Cyber Risk Guidance for Financial Firms

On 19 November, the Bank of England, PRA and FCA jointly published AI cyber "effective practices" covering prompt injections, model poisoning, adversarial inputs, and deepfakes. AI is "materially reducing the cost and skills" needed to attack financial systems. Formal consultation closes 26 February 2027.

Why It Matters

These are present obligations, not future concerns. Any product touching client data or financial transactions needs a cyber risk section in its design from day one.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Every day you build products for people who operate in these markets — and speak this language. The more fluent you become, the more credible you are as a product partner. Six terms to own this week.

Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. A liquid market means tight bid-ask spreads and fast execution. Illiquid assets (e.g. small-cap stocks, private credit) are harder to trade quickly. AI tools that surface liquidity signals are genuinely high-value for traders.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares, selling them at today's price, and hoping to buy them back cheaper later — profiting from a decline. Short sellers often do intensive research to find overvalued companies. AI tools for short-side research are a growing product category.
Market Capitalisation
Share price multiplied by total shares outstanding. It determines whether a company is small-cap (under ~$2B), mid-cap, or large-cap ($10B+). Analysts screen by market cap constantly — your product's filters likely expose it.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net profit divided by shares outstanding. Higher EPS generally signals a more profitable company per share. Analysts compare EPS to consensus forecasts — a "beat" or "miss" on EPS is a major market-moving event, and AI tools that track earnings surprises are widely used.
Derivatives
Contracts whose value derives from an underlying asset — options, futures, and swaps are all derivatives. Hedge funds use them to hedge risk or take leveraged positions. AI is transforming derivatives pricing and risk management across major banks.
Benchmark Index
A standard against which portfolio returns are measured — the S&P 500 or FTSE 100 are common examples. "Beating the benchmark" is the fundamental goal. AI systems are now benchmarked against human analysts — just as your product will be measured against user baselines.

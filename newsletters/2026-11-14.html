Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

This week's sell-off is a live lesson in how markets price risk and sentiment. The vocabulary below is what your portfolio managers and analysts use to interpret moments like this — and the terms you need to participate credibly in those conversations.

Beta
Measures a stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta of 1 moves with the index; above 1 is more volatile. AI and semiconductor stocks have high betas — a small index move produces a large price swing.
Sharpe Ratio
Return per unit of risk taken. Calculated as (portfolio return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation. The higher the Sharpe, the better the risk-adjusted performance. AI tools must demonstrate this metric to earn PM trust.
Basis Points (bps)
One basis point equals 0.01%. Used by analysts to describe rate and spread changes with precision. When credit spreads widen by "50 bps," borrowing costs have risen by 0.5%. Never say "half a percent" in a rates conversation.
Market Maker
A firm that continuously quotes both buy and sell prices for a security, providing liquidity to the market. AI is transforming how market makers price risk in real time — a key dynamic in AI-driven trading volatility.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
Total fund assets minus liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. Calculated daily at asset managers. Errors in NAV calculation are costly compliance failures — AI automation of this process is now common.
Liquidity Premium
The extra return investors demand for holding an asset that cannot be quickly sold. AI portfolio recommendations that ignore liquidity create execution risk — a reason why buy-side AI tools need market microstructure awareness.

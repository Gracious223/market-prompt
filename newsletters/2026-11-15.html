73% of Fund Managers Use GenAI for Investment Research — Analysts Named the Most Exposed Role

AIMA's latest survey finds genAI now embedded in investment decision-making across the majority of fund managers — but respondents drew a distinction: analysts, whose work centres on data synthesis and first-draft research, are seen as far more exposed to displacement than portfolio managers, whose edge sits in judgement and relationships.

Why It Matters

Your users are the people whose jobs are changing fastest. Building tools that make analysts faster — rather than tools that make them feel replaced — is a product strategy choice, not a technical one.

Research & Think About
  • Map the analyst workflow from data collection through to investment memo — where does AI currently assist, and where is it missing?
  • Read the AIMA "Artificial Realities" report — it benchmarks AI adoption across research, risk, and operations functions
  • Explainability matters most for investment decisions, per AIMA respondents — how does your product communicate confidence and uncertainty?

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your analysts talk in a shorthand that took them years to acquire. The six terms below cover market microstructure, risk measurement, and research economics — areas where AI products are creating the most visible change right now.

Tracking Error
How much a portfolio's returns deviate from its benchmark. A low tracking error means tight index-hugging; a high one signals active bets. AI-driven portfolios are scrutinised on this metric — it quantifies the cost of departing from the benchmark.
Dark Pools
Private electronic trading venues where large institutional orders are matched without displaying prices publicly. Used to avoid moving the market on a large trade. AI is now used to decide when to route orders to dark pools versus lit exchanges.
T+1 Settlement
Trades in US equities now settle the business day after execution. The UK moves to T+1 in 2027. Faster settlement reduces counterparty risk but demands tighter operational automation — a live use case for AI in post-trade processing.
Convexity
The second-order sensitivity of a bond's price to interest rate changes — how much duration itself changes as rates move. Bonds with positive convexity benefit more from rate falls than they lose from rate rises. AI tools modelling fixed income portfolios must account for it.
Soft Dollars
Commission credits earned when buy-side firms execute trades through brokers, then used to pay for research services. AI research tools are disrupting the soft-dollar model — if AI replaces broker research, the rationale for the credit arrangement weakens.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value over a given period. Maximum drawdown is a standard risk metric for portfolio managers. AI products that surface drawdown risk in real time — before it materialises — are among the most credible value propositions in the space.

BNY: Point-Solution Agents Deliver Only Linear Gains — Systemic Integration Is the Real Prize

BNY's latest research argues that isolated AI agents — automating single tasks — produce linear cost savings but cannot resolve fragmented data or inconsistent workflows. The buy-side firms seeing transformational gains are those connecting agents across data infrastructure, not bolting them onto existing processes.

Why It Matters

This is the architectural choice your product team will face. A workflow-integrated tool that touches multiple data sources is harder to build but far harder to displace than a one-task chatbot.

  • Map the data handoffs in your product: where does data come from, where does it go, and where does it break? Those breaks are your integration opportunities
  • Research what "event-driven architecture" means — it is the infrastructure pattern that makes real-time agentic finance possible

BoE · FCA · Joint Survey

BoE/FCA Survey: 80% of UK Financial Firms Have AI Agents in Production — Only 2% Allow Broad Autonomy

The BoE and FCA's fourth annual AI survey (26 October 2026) found 86% of firms use AI — up from 75% in 2024. AI agents are deployed at 80% of respondents, mostly for decision support. But consequential autonomous actions — trade execution, payment approval, customer outcomes — remain rare: fewer than 9% plan them within 12 months.

Why It Matters

The adoption numbers are impressive. The autonomy numbers reveal the actual constraint: firms are waiting for governance frameworks and regulatory clarity before they let agents act, not waiting for better models.

Research & Think About
  • Read the full BoE/FCA report — the third-party AI oversight section (39% of deployments are third-party) is directly relevant to any product you build on external models
  • Understand what "constrained execution" vs. "conditional autonomy" vs. "broad autonomy" means in the survey's typology — it maps directly to your product's decision-making design
  • Note that 94% of respondents have firm-wide AI policies — your product needs to fit inside those, not around them

FCA · Good Practice Guide

FCA Tells Firms: Map Existing Rules to Your AI Tools — Don't Wait for New Ones

In its October 19 good practice publication — drawing on its AI Live Testing programme — the FCA made clear it expects firms to demonstrate how Consumer Duty, SM&CR, and operational resilience requirements apply to their AI systems now. Six of the seven Mills Review recommendations are being acted on.

Why It Matters

"We're waiting for AI rules" is no longer a valid posture. Your next PRD needs an accountability section: which Senior Manager Function owns the outputs your product produces, and what is the consumer outcome standard it must meet?

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's good and poor practice guide — focus on the governance and consumer outcome sections, not the technology sections
  • Understand what "Senior Management Function" (SMF) means operationally — this is the accountable individual if your AI tool produces a harmful output
  • Draft a one-page accountability mapping for your product: AI output → decision type → regulatory obligation → accountable person

Role Evolution

Agentic AI Is Rewriting the PM Job Description — Orchestration Is Now the Core Skill

As agentic systems replace simple chatbot features, the PM role is shifting from managing feature backlogs to orchestrating AI agents: defining what agents can decide, what they must escalate, and how outputs are verified. Vibe coding is lowering the barrier to prototyping — PMs can now test hypotheses without waiting for engineers.

Why It Matters

"Orchestration" in a regulated context means you're the one defining the guardrails. That's both a responsibility and a leverage point — the PM who can design an agentic workflow with clear human-in-the-loop touchpoints earns trust from compliance and from users simultaneously.

  • Define what "human-in-the-loop" means specifically for your product: is it review before action, review before publish, or override after the fact?

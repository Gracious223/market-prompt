Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

This week's terms are anchored to today's sell-off and buy-side context — vocabulary that comes up directly when analysts discuss the Nasdaq correction, Intel's guidance miss, and why Ridgeline's AUM figure matters for product positioning.

Market Capitalisation
Total value of a company's outstanding shares: share price × shares issued. When Alphabet fell 12%, hundreds of billions in market cap were erased in a single session. It's how "size" is measured in public markets.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough percentage decline in a portfolio or index. The Nasdaq is down 7.8% from its October 6 all-time high — that's a drawdown. AI-heavy portfolios have experienced steeper drawdowns this month.
Earnings Guidance
Management's forward-looking forecast for revenue or profit in coming quarters. Intel's Q4 guidance miss drove its 30% single-day fall. Your product's value lives or dies by helping analysts anticipate guidance surprises before they happen.
AUM — Assets Under Management
The total value of assets a fund or platform oversees on behalf of clients. Ridgeline has $650B+ in committed AUM. AUM defines the scale of the buy-side market your product serves — it tells you the stakes of every analyst decision.
Liquidity Risk
The risk of being unable to sell an asset quickly without materially moving its price. During a sector sell-off like this week's, liquidity risk rises: heavy volume amplifies price moves and makes orderly exits harder to execute.
Sharpe Ratio
A risk-adjusted return measure: return above the risk-free rate, divided by volatility. A higher Sharpe means better return per unit of risk taken. AI screening tools are increasingly benchmarked this way — it's the standard your users judge investment decisions against.

Denmark's First AI-Agent Payment — Mastercard and Danske Bank Mark a Milestone

Mastercard and Danske Bank completed what is reported as Denmark's first payment made entirely by an AI agent in early October 2026. No human initiated the transaction: the agent identified the payment obligation and executed it autonomously. A first for a regulated European financial institution operating under PSD2.

Why It Matters

This is the proof point that agentic finance has crossed the regulatory threshold into live production. The question for your product is no longer "will agents be allowed?" — it is "what will your agents be authorised to do, and who signs off?"

FSB Finalises 12 Sound Practices for Responsible AI in Finance — Boards Now Directly Accountable

The Financial Stability Board published its final report on October 15, setting 12 sound practices for responsible AI adoption by financial institutions. The framework places board-level governance, clear accountability structures, and adaptable risk frameworks at the top. Non-prescriptive by design, the FSB positions these as global baseline expectations — not an international standard — reflecting consultation feedback from the GFMA and other industry bodies.

Why It Matters

When a board is formally accountable for AI risk, every product decision you make is one step closer to a board-level conversation. Building an audit trail into your product architecture is no longer optional governance hygiene — it is a board-level demand from the FSB down.

Core Competency

Designing Failure States: The Most Underrated AI PM Skill of 2026

Roadmaps Conference's 2026 AI PM guide names "designing failure states" — defining exactly what the product does when the model is wrong — as the most underrated skill in the field. In a high-stakes domain like investment analysis, the tolerance for silent errors is near-zero. How a product fails gracefully is a product decision, not a model one — and it belongs in the spec before the model is ever chosen.

Why It Matters

Your portfolio managers will not forgive a confident wrong answer. The product that earns trust in finance is the one that knows its own limits — and communicates them clearly before the user acts on a bad output.

Research & Think About
  • Read the Roadmaps 2026 AI PM guide — the failure states section is the most directly applicable to a high-accountability financial context
  • For your current product, list three ways the model could fail silently — outputting something wrong without any visible signal. What does the product do in each case?
  • Research "calibration" in machine learning — a well-calibrated model knows when it doesn't know. Ask your engineers how you'd measure calibration in your product's outputs

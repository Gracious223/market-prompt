Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

This week's terms cover the language of earnings and market structure — vocabulary you need when Alphabet is falling 12% and your portfolio managers are watching every tick. These show up daily in the reports your analysts read.

EPS — Earnings Per Share
A company's net profit divided by its number of shares. The core number analysts compare to expectations. When Alphabet "missed" on earnings, they meant EPS came in below consensus estimates.
P/E Ratio — Price to Earnings
How much investors pay per £/$ of earnings. A high P/E means the market expects strong future growth. AI stocks often trade at high P/E multiples — which is why missing earnings expectations hurts so much.
VIX — Volatility Index
The "fear gauge" — it measures expected 30-day volatility in S&P 500 options. A rising VIX signals market anxiety. AI-linked earnings misses can spike the VIX across the sector, not just the single stock.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares and selling them, hoping to buy them back cheaper later. "Short sellers" profit when stocks fall. High short interest in an AI stock is a signal that some investors expect it to disappoint on earnings.
Derivatives — Options & Futures
Financial contracts whose value derives from an underlying asset. Options give the right (not obligation) to buy or sell. Portfolio managers use derivatives to hedge risk or take leveraged positions on AI-exposed stocks.
Sector Rotation
When investors shift capital between sectors (e.g. from tech to energy or financials). An AI earnings reckoning can trigger rotation out of high-multiple tech — affecting every stock in the sector, even ones that delivered.

arXiv: AI Agents Debating Buy and Sell Signals Beat Conventional Screening on Sharpe

A 2026 paper (arXiv 2603.23300) tested a multi-agent portfolio screening system where specialist agents argue opposing signals before a decision is reached. The setup outperformed both unscreened baselines and conventional factor screening on risk-adjusted returns, using only public S&P 500 data.

Why It Matters

The "debate" architecture — where agents argue before acting — is a pattern worth knowing. It's a practical model for building AI that your PMs can interrogate, not just accept. Explainability by design, not bolt-on.

Lead Regulatory Story

FCA Plans to Become an 'Agentic Regulator' — Building Rules for Finance's AI Agents

In an October 19 speech, FCA Chief Data and Intelligence Officer Jessica Rusu set out a plan to build an "agentic finance" oversight framework in partnership with industry, other regulators, and AI labs. The FCA will use AI in its own supervision — analysing regulatory returns at scale — and is considering extending incident reporting obligations to significant AI failures. No new AI-specific rules are planned; the FCA will rely on existing frameworks while building agentic-specific guidance.

Why It Matters

Your regulator is building AI agents to supervise your AI agents. Your product's audit trail must be designed for machine-readable inspection, not just human review — the FCA's supervisory tools will eventually be able to query your model outputs directly.

A transatlantic split is emerging: the FCA is building new agentic finance governance infrastructure, while the SEC folds AI into its existing examination framework and flags frontier AI's systemic risk.

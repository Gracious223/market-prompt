Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's AI selloff story turns on specific mechanics of how hedge funds manage risk. These six terms explain what's happening — and why your product might matter more in a drawdown than in a rally.

Long/Short Equity
A hedge fund strategy that buys stocks expected to rise (long) and sells borrowed stocks expected to fall (short). The netting of these positions is designed to reduce market exposure.
Gross Exposure
Total longs plus total shorts, expressed as a percentage of capital. A fund cutting gross exposure is reducing the size of its entire book — both sides — not just selling long positions.
Net Exposure
Longs minus shorts. A fund with 80% long and 30% short has 50% net exposure. High net means more market directionality; hedge funds manage this carefully.
Deleveraging
Reducing borrowed capital and overall position sizes. When a fund deleverages, it typically sells assets and pays back margin loans — often causing price pressure in crowded trades.
Prime Brokerage
The Goldman or Morgan Stanley desk that provides hedge funds with leverage, custody, and securities lending. Prime brokers hold unique data on positioning — closely watched by the market.
Short Covering
Buying back borrowed shares to close a short position. If prices rise, short sellers must cover to limit losses — which paradoxically drives prices higher. Distinguishing this from genuine buying matters.

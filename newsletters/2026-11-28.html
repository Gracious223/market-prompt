AIMA Survey: Buy-Side Sees Analysts as AI's Primary Target — Not Portfolio Managers

74% of buy-side professionals surveyed by AIMA believe portfolio managers are the role least likely to be disrupted by generative AI. Analysts — who produce the structured research PMs act on — are seen as far more exposed. AIMA flags a deeper question: if junior analysts are displaced, how do future PMs develop the investment judgement they need?

Why It Matters

Your users (analysts) are the ones most anxious about AI — and most motivated to adopt it. That anxiety is both your biggest adoption lever and your biggest UX risk. Build tools that make analysts faster and more credible, not tools that make them feel replaceable.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your users think in these terms every day. Building fluency in them helps you ask better discovery questions, write sharper PRDs, and earn credibility in the room. Six concepts to add to your vocabulary this week.

Short Selling
Borrowing a security you don't own and selling it, betting the price will fall. You profit if it drops before you must return the shares. High-risk; losses are theoretically unlimited.
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between the highest price a buyer will pay (bid) and the lowest a seller will accept (ask). A narrow spread signals high liquidity; a wide spread signals thin markets or higher transaction cost.
Beta
A measure of a stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta of 1 moves in line with the market; above 1 is more volatile; below 1 is less. AI tools are increasingly used to model factor exposures including beta.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a specific period. A core risk metric — portfolio managers track maximum drawdown closely when stress-testing AI-generated recommendations.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
Total fund assets minus liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. The per-share value of a fund. For ETFs, it's calculated daily; for mutual funds, at market close. Critical for fair pricing of fund units.
Tracking Error
How much a portfolio's returns deviate from its benchmark index. Low tracking error = closely follows index. AI-driven portfolio optimisation tools often target specific tracking error budgets for passive strategies.

FCA · UK · Agentic AI

FCA Sets Out Its Approach to Agentic AI: Senior Managers Are Accountable for Third-Party Agents

The FCA published "Our approach to Agentic AI" on November 24, applying no new rules — only existing SM&CR accountability. The core position: when an AI agent moves money or makes investment decisions, the boundary of regulated activity "is set by Parliament," and the named senior manager is personally accountable, regardless of whether the agent is third-party.

Why It Matters

Your product's outputs may flow into regulated decision-making processes. If a named senior manager is accountable for every agentic action, your product's auditability and explainability become their compliance tools. Build accordingly.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's "Our approach to Agentic AI" (November 24, 2026) in full — it's short
  • Under SM&CR: who at your firm would be named as accountable for your product's outputs?
  • What audit trail does your product currently produce? Is it sufficient for a senior manager to defend a decision?

PRA · FCA · Bank of England

UK Regulators Issue Joint AI Cyber-Risk Practices for Financial Firms

On November 19, the PRA, FCA, and Bank of England jointly published non-binding practices on AI cyber risk and resilience. Named threats include prompt injection, model poisoning, adversarial inputs, jailbreaking, autonomous agent attacks, and deepfakes — described as "complementary to formal supervisory expectations," not mandatory.

Why It Matters

Prompt injection and model poisoning are product-level risks, not just IT risks. If you're building on top of LLMs, your threat model must include these — and your PRD should address them.

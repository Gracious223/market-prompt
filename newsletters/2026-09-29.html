Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers use daily — each with a direct connection to how AI tools are reshaping the workflow around them.

Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value over a period. Maximum drawdown is a key risk metric — AI tools that monitor it in real time allow PMs to act faster than traditional weekly reporting cycles allow.
Yield Curve
A graph of bond yields vs. maturity lengths. An "inverted" curve (short rates above long) historically signals recession risk. AI now parses yield curve shape to flag macro regime shifts and brief PMs automatically.
Credit Spread
The yield premium a corporate bond pays above a risk-free government bond of equivalent maturity. Tightening spreads signal market confidence; widening spreads signal stress. AI credit tools monitor spread dynamics for early warning signals.
Active vs. Passive
Active managers select securities to beat the market (higher fees); passive funds track an index at low cost. AI is disrupting both: enabling cheaper active strategies and smarter "smart beta" tilts within passive structures.
Tracking Error
The standard deviation of a portfolio's returns relative to its benchmark. Low = hugging the index. High = active bets. A key metric for evaluating AI-assisted portfolio construction — and how much risk a PM is willing to own.
Short Selling
Borrowing and selling an asset with the intent to buy it back cheaper. Shorts require precise timing and conviction — an area where AI sentiment analysis, news parsing, and alternative data tools are increasingly used to find short ideas.

The FCA published a major speech signalling that it will maintain its principles-based AI approach: innovate freely, but Consumer Duty and SM&CR still govern every AI output. The Mills Review, examining how AI reshapes retail financial services, is expected to report imminently — watch for enforcement posture shifts.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers use daily — different from every previous edition. Each one will come up in conversations with the people your product serves.

Yield Curve
A plot of bond yields across different maturities. An inverted yield curve (short rates above long rates) is a classic recession signal. PMs and macro analysts watch it constantly — it shapes asset allocation across the whole portfolio.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to changes in interest rates — measured in years. A duration of 7 means the bond loses ~7% in value for every 1% rise in rates. It's the core risk metric for fixed income portfolios and shapes how PMs position in different rate environments.
Market Capitalisation
A company's total equity value: share price × shares outstanding. Used to classify companies as large-cap, mid-cap, or small-cap. Different cap buckets have different risk/return profiles — and different data coverage, which matters for your AI product's performance.
P/E Ratio
Price-to-earnings: how many times a company's annual earnings investors are willing to pay for one share. The simplest valuation signal. A high P/E means the market expects strong future growth; a low P/E may signal value — or value trap.
Sharpe Ratio
A measure of risk-adjusted return: (return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation. A Sharpe above 1 is good; above 2 is exceptional. PMs use it to compare strategies on equal footing — it's how AI-driven strategies prove they're generating real edge, not just taking more risk.
Credit Spread
The extra yield a corporate bond pays over a comparable government bond, compensating for default risk. Spreads widen in stress (investors demand more risk premium) and tighten in calm markets. A key indicator analysts track to gauge credit market health and risk appetite.

FCA Rethinks Regulation for the Age of AI — No New Rules, But the Framework Is Evolving

The FCA has published a speech setting out its updated position: existing frameworks — Consumer Duty, SM&CR, operational resilience — are sufficient to govern AI. The ongoing Mills Review into how AI transforms retail financial services is expected to shape guidance by year-end. The message to firms: innovate, but own the outcomes.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your portfolio managers and analysts live inside these concepts daily. Here are six terms that come up constantly in buy-side conversations — different from last edition's set, designed to build your vocabulary one rotation at a time.

Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Illiquid assets (private credit, small-cap equities) carry a premium — and a risk. AI tools that surface liquidity signals are particularly valuable in stressed markets.
Benchmark Index
A reference index (e.g. S&P 500, MSCI World, Bloomberg Aggregate) against which a portfolio's returns are measured. Understanding which benchmark a fund tracks is essential context for any AI product built for PMs.
Short Selling
Borrowing and selling a security you don't own, betting the price will fall. You buy it back later at a lower price to profit. A core strategy for hedge funds — and a key signal for sentiment-based AI analytics.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value over a period. A 20% drawdown means the portfolio fell 20% from its highest point before recovering. A key risk metric PMs use to evaluate strategy resilience.
Systematic vs. Discretionary
Systematic strategies follow quantitative rules with minimal human override. Discretionary relies on a manager's judgment. Most AI products are targeting the systematic space — but the real opportunity may be in augmenting discretionary judgment.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
A company's net profit divided by outstanding shares. A core metric analysts track quarterly. "Beat or miss" on EPS often moves a stock materially — making earnings season one of the highest-value periods for your AI product to prove itself.

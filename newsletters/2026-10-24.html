Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Each edition rotates six terms your analysts and portfolio managers use daily. Build this vocabulary and you'll hold your own in any investment team conversation.

Beta
A stock's sensitivity to broad market moves. Beta of 1.0 = moves with the market. Above 1.0 = amplifies moves (typical for high-growth AI stocks). AI risk tools use beta to monitor portfolio-level market exposure in real time.
Yield Curve
A plot of interest rates at different maturities (e.g. 2-year vs. 10-year). An inverted curve (short rates above long rates) historically signals a recession — and is one of the macro signals AI market intelligence tools are trained to flag first.
Basis Points (bps)
The standard unit for interest rate changes. 1 basis point = 0.01%. A "25 bps rate cut" = 0.25%. Analysts use bps to avoid ambiguity — "a 1% change in a 2% rate" is very different depending on whether you mean additive or relative.
Max Drawdown
The largest peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio over a period. A key risk metric: a fund with a 40% max drawdown has twice the risk tolerance required of one with 20%. AI risk systems are increasingly used to forecast drawdown before it happens.
P/E Ratio
Price divided by earnings per share. The most common equity valuation multiple. A P/E of 30 means investors pay $30 for every $1 of earnings. AI-heavy stocks often trade at triple-digit P/Es — the Anthropic IPO debate is ultimately a P/E argument.
Free Float
The proportion of a company's shares available for public trading — excludes locked-up founder or institutional stakes. A low free float amplifies price moves. AI IPOs typically list a small free float initially, increasing volatility in early trading.

Gartner · Finance Automation

Gartner: 15% of Day-to-Day Finance Decisions Will Be Made Autonomously by AI Agents by 2028

Gartner's 2026 guidance for CFOs projects that at least 15% of routine finance decisions will be made by AI agents without human review by 2028 — up from near-zero today. Only 31% of finance organisations plan to build agent capabilities in-house; the majority are buying through existing platforms. Governance is the bottleneck.

Why It Matters

The "15% autonomous" figure is low enough to sound safe — but it covers high-frequency, high-volume decisions. Your product needs to draw a clear line between decisions the agent makes and decisions the analyst makes, and that line must survive regulatory scrutiny.

Research & Think About
  • Read Gartner's full CFO agentic AI guidance — focus on the governance section, specifically the concept of a "pre-approved decision list"
  • Map the decisions in your analysts' daily workflow: which are repeatable enough to be candidates for autonomous execution?
  • Research "human-in-the-loop" vs. "human-on-the-loop" — two different architectures, very different regulatory implications

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms that come up constantly in conversations with portfolio managers and analysts — and that you need to understand to build products that earn their trust.

NAV (Net Asset Value)
The total value of a fund's assets minus liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. For ETFs, it is calculated daily. AI-managed ETFs like FINQ's AIUP are priced relative to their NAV.
Beta
A measure of how much a security moves relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with the market. Beta of 2 = twice as volatile. Used to quantify market risk in AI-generated strategies.
Short Selling
Borrowing a security, selling it, and hoping to repurchase it at a lower price. FINQ's AINT ETF uses short selling, introducing theoretically unlimited downside — a key risk disclosure.
Active vs. Passive Management
Passive funds track an index (e.g. S&P 500) with minimal intervention. Active managers try to outperform. AI-managed funds sit in an emerging third category — algorithmic active management.
Market Microstructure
The mechanics of how trades are executed: order types, bid-ask spreads, liquidity, and market impact. The FCA's wholesale banks review flagged algorithmic trading controls in this layer.
Sector Rotation
Shifting capital between industry sectors (tech, financials, energy, etc.) based on the economic cycle. AI tools that help PMs time sector moves are among the highest-velocity use cases in 2026.

BoE & FCA: 86% of Financial Firms Run AI in Production — Only 2% Allow Broad Agent Autonomy

The fourth annual BoE/FCA joint survey (196 respondents, published 26 Oct 2026) confirms near-universal production AI adoption. Among large firms, 94% run at least one live AI system. GenAI now accounts for 56% of reported systems. Yet only 2% allow agents to act with broad autonomy. Third-party AI systems make up 53% of high-materiality deployments, up from 35% in 2024.

Why It Matters

The industry has moved from piloting to producing — but it has not moved from narrow tools to autonomous agents. The 2% autonomy ceiling is a product constraint and a business opportunity at the same time.

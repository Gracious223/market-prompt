Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's terms are grounded in valuation, positioning, and market dynamics — the language your portfolio managers use when they evaluate both the equities they hold and the tools they choose to analyse them. These six come up constantly in analyst conversations and investment committee meetings.

Short Selling
Borrowing shares and selling them, betting the price will fall. If it does, you buy them back cheaper and pocket the difference. AI is increasingly used to identify short opportunities — understanding this helps you grasp a key use case for your product.
Yield Curve
A graph plotting interest rates on bonds of the same credit quality across different maturities. An "inverted" yield curve (short-term rates higher than long-term) has historically preceded recessions. Macro analysts monitor it daily — your product's macro intelligence features live here.
Basis Points (bps)
One hundredth of one percentage point. If a rate moves from 4.00% to 4.25%, it moved 25 basis points. Analysts speak in basis points to be precise about small changes that matter enormously at scale. You will hear "bps" constantly — never confuse it with percentage points.
Beta
A measure of how much a stock moves relative to the broader market. A beta of 1.5 means the stock moves 50% more than the index — up or down. High-beta stocks are riskier; low-beta are more defensive. AI screening tools often rank portfolios by beta as a first-pass risk filter.
Sector Rotation
The movement of capital from one market sector to another as economic conditions change. As AI stocks sell off, capital rotates into defensive sectors like utilities. Your product's signals are most valuable during rotation events — they create the most decision pressure on your users.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
Share price divided by earnings per share. A high P/E means investors are paying more for each dollar of earnings — either because they expect growth or because the stock is expensive. AI companies often carry elevated P/E ratios. Analysts use P/E to compare valuations across peers.

Six Major Banks Demand Audit Trails as AI Shopping Agents Prepare to Spend Autonomously

A coalition including Bank of America, Capital One, NatWest, ASB, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, and ING warned that AI shopping agents could expose card details and weaken payment security — then called for mandatory audit trails from consumer instruction to final payment outcome. Why It Matters

When major banks demand audit trails for consumer AI agents, they're signalling the same expectation for institutional AI tools. The governance model being negotiated for agentic commerce will set the template for agentic finance broadly — including the products you are building.

Lead Regulatory Story

FCA Builds Its AI Supervisory Apparatus — Six of Seven Mills Review Recommendations Now in Live Work

In an October 19, 2026 speech, the FCA announced it is moving six of seven Mills Review recommendations into live supervisory work rather than further consultation. The regulator is building an agentic tool to supervise approximately 9,000 small wholesale transaction-reporting firms. Key supervisory priorities going forward: agent identity, delegated authority frameworks, and continuous AI assurance — not a new AI rulebook. The FCA's framing is clear: existing SM&CR and Consumer Duty rules are sufficient. The apparatus to apply them to AI is what's being built now.

Why It Matters

The FCA isn't waiting for new legislation — it is building the infrastructure to supervise AI under existing rules. If your product is deployed in a UK-regulated context, the accountability framework being built right now is the one your product will be examined against. Agent identity and delegated authority are product decisions, not compliance afterthoughts.

Research & Think About
  • Read the bitsofblocks analysis of the FCA's October 19 speech — it is the clearest plain-language summary of what the FCA's AI supervisory roadmap actually requires of firms
  • What does "agent identity" mean in a regulatory context? If an AI agent acts on behalf of a senior manager, who is the accountable person under SM&CR?
  • The FCA is building its own agentic supervision tool — what does it mean for your product design that the regulator itself is now an agentic AI user?

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's stories turn on the mechanics of how asset managers measure performance, manage risk, and make decisions. These six terms are the vocabulary of the desk your tools serve — understand them and you will ask better questions in discovery.

AUM
Assets Under Management — the total market value of assets a firm manages on behalf of clients. It is the primary measure of a fund manager's scale. When AI tools save a PM time, the value proposition is often framed in AUM per analyst.
Basis Points (bps)
1/100th of a percentage point. 25 bps = 0.25%. Used everywhere in finance to avoid ambiguity over percentages. If a strategy adds 50 bps of alpha, it outperforms its benchmark by 0.5%. Knowing this avoids a glaring gap in credibility.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio or asset price. Max drawdown measures the worst drop from a peak before recovery. AI risk tools often focus on drawdown prediction — a manager's key concern in a selloff.
Sector Rotation
Shifting capital between market sectors (e.g., moving from tech to energy). The AI selloff is driving rotation out of AI-heavy positions into defensives. Tools that flag sector concentration and rotation signals are high-value right now.
Rebalancing
Restoring a portfolio to its target weights after market moves change the composition. AI is increasingly used to identify rebalancing signals and model the tax and transaction cost implications before execution.
Tracking Error
The standard deviation of a portfolio's returns versus its benchmark. Low tracking error means the portfolio closely follows its index; high means it's making active bets. AI outputs that increase unintended tracking error are a product risk.

Sovos and EY both argue that agentic orchestration can run AP, invoice matching, approval routing, and payment end to end — with EY estimating up to 40% cost savings. Why It Matters

The Messy Middle

Where Agentic AI Actually Works in Finance — And Where It Breaks

MostlyMetrics' widely shared analysis identifies the "messy middle" in finance: AI agents that work well on structured, bounded tasks (reconciliation, data extraction, structured reports) but fail on ambiguous, multi-source judgment calls. The key variable is data structure, not model capability.

Why It Matters

The highest-failure agents in finance aren't bad models — they're agents built on messy data. Your product's reliability is a data infrastructure problem first, an AI problem second.

Research & Think About
  • Audit the data sources your planned agent will touch — what percentage are structured vs. unstructured?
  • Read: "Reliability in LLM applications" — what causes agentic failures in practice?
  • Identify one tightly bounded task at your firm where data is clean — that is your first agent pilot candidate

FCA · Agentic Finance

FCA Commits to a Trusted Framework for Agentic Finance — Near-Miss Reporting a Priority

A November 14 commentary (Ecaveo) on the FCA's October good practice publication reveals a significant forward commitment: the FCA said agentic systems require "continuous, conditional assurance," named near-miss reporting as a regulatory priority, and committed to developing a trusted framework specifically for agentic finance. This is the first signal that agentic AI will get its own regulatory treatment.

Why It Matters

Near-miss reporting for AI agents means your product needs an audit trail for every agent action — not just outcomes. "Continuous, conditional assurance" is a product requirement, not a compliance checkbox.

Research & Think About
  • Read the Ecaveo November 14 commentary — it unpacks what "continuous, conditional assurance" means for a board
  • What would near-miss reporting look like in your product — what events would trigger a log entry?
  • Learn how the FCA's existing SM&CR accountability maps to agentic AI — who is the Senior Manager for an agent action?

The SEC's Fiscal Year 2026 Examination Priorities explicitly flag AI as an exam focus — in particular, the accuracy of registrants' claims about AI capabilities in marketing and client communications.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

It is Q3 earnings season — the period when listed companies report quarterly results and CEOs make their most visible public statements about strategy, including AI. Here are six terms you will encounter this week across analyst calls and market commentary.

Earnings Season
The four-week period each quarter when most public companies report financial results. Q3 runs mid-October. Analysts update models; share prices often move sharply on surprises.
P/E Ratio
Price divided by earnings per share. A P/E of 30 means investors pay $30 for every $1 of profit. High P/E signals growth expectations — AI stocks often trade at elevated multiples.
Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding. Defines large-cap (over $10B), mid-cap, and small-cap. Most AI infrastructure plays sit at mega-cap. Analysts segment coverage by market cap.
Sector Rotation
When investors move capital from one sector to another — e.g. from technology into financials. AI is driving unusual flows as both a tech and an infrastructure story simultaneously.
Earnings Beat / Miss
Whether a company's reported earnings exceeded or fell short of analyst consensus estimates. A "beat" on both revenue and EPS typically drives the share price up on results day.
ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)
A basket of securities that trades on an exchange like a single stock. AI-themed ETFs (e.g. tracking AI infrastructure or application companies) let investors express thematic views cheaply.

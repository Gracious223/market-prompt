Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six terms your analysts use daily — particularly relevant this week as credit spreads widen, AI capex debates intensify, and the question of what drives stock rallies versus corrections becomes central to your users' work.

Yield Curve
A graph showing interest rates across different bond maturities. A "flattening" yield curve — shorter and longer rates converging — signals investor uncertainty about growth. AI infrastructure financing costs are directly sensitive to where the curve sits.
Beta
A measure of how much a stock moves relative to the broader market. A beta of 1.5 means the stock moves 50% more than the index. High-beta AI stocks amplify both rallies and selloffs — relevant to every AI infrastructure name your PMs track.
Credit Spread
The extra yield a corporate bond pays over a government bond of the same maturity. Spreads widen when investors demand more compensation for risk. Barclays flagged widening AI credit spreads this week as a potential buy opportunity — that framing assumes spreads will compress again.
Capex vs. Opex
Capital expenditure (capex) is spending on long-lived assets — data centres, chips. Operational expenditure (opex) is day-to-day running costs. The AI spending debate is fundamentally a capex question: are these investments generating returns fast enough to justify the capital deployed?
Market Makers
Firms that quote both buy and sell prices for a security, providing liquidity. HSBC's CEO claim that AI agents could replace human traders within 2–4 years is fundamentally a market-making story — the task is highly rules-based and data-intensive, making it susceptible to automation.
Leverage
Using borrowed money to amplify investment returns — and losses. Concerns about "debt-funded AI investment" this week refer to leverage: firms borrowing to build AI infrastructure. Rising rates increase the cost of that leverage and can trigger forced selling if assets decline.

Kognitos Launches 61 "AI Employees" for Finance — Each Trained on 515 Standard Operating Procedures

Kognitos introduced role-based AI agents targeting accounts payable, reconciliation, month-end close, and KYC work. Each of the 61 agents follows 515 standard operating procedures. Why It Matters

Research & Think About

  • Compare Kognitos' governance model (human approval for any new SOP) to your own product's approval chain — is your escalation path as explicit?

FCA · 19–20 October 2026

FCA Review: AI Governance Is Lagging Behind Deployment Across All Financial Sectors

The FCA published its AI good-practice review on October 19, covering ML, generative AI, and agentic AI use cases. It found three consistent failure modes: oversight retrofitted after deployment rather than built in; accountability too diffuse to trace; and monitoring insufficient once models go live. The FCA continues to rely on Consumer Duty, SM&CR, and SYSC rather than AI-specific rules.

Why It Matters

"Retrofitted oversight" is the FCA's term for what happens when product teams deploy and ask compliance to catch up. If your product launches without a governance design, you are already behind the FCA's minimum expectation — even under principles-based regulation.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's full good-practice publication — note the sections on agentic AI specifically, where accountability questions become most acute
  • Understand what "diffuse accountability" means under SM&CR — identify which Senior Manager function at your firm would be accountable for an AI output that causes a poor consumer outcome
  • Audit your product's governance documentation against the three FCA failure modes — if you cannot answer each one clearly, that is your compliance gap

Meta · 15 October 2026

Meta Launches Open-Weight Muse Spark: 816B Parameters, 1 Million Token Context — Available to Any Builder

Meta launched Muse Spark Open on October 15 — an open-weight model with 816 billion parameters and a one-million token context window. Open-weight means any organisation can download and run it inside its own infrastructure without routing data through an external API. At this scale, it is the largest openly available model to date and directly challenges the closed-API model that OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google rely on commercially.

Why It Matters

For a buy-side firm concerned about proprietary data leaving its perimeter, a 1M-token open-weight model that runs on-premise is a genuine architectural alternative. That changes the data privacy argument for internal AI products — and shifts the build-vs-buy calculus again.

Research & Think About
  • Understand what "open-weight" means practically — the model weights are publicly available, so a firm runs them on its own hardware with no API dependency and no data egress risk
  • Learn why a 1M-token context window matters for financial analysis — an entire earnings transcript, full 10-K, and broker note can fit in a single prompt, enabling analysis that is impossible with smaller windows
  • Ask your infrastructure team: what would it cost to run an 816B parameter model on-premise, and at what query volume does it become cheaper than an external API?

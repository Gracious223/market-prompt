Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Your users live in the language of markets. These six terms come up daily in analyst and portfolio manager workflows — and in the product decisions you will have to make about what your AI surfaces, calculates, and flags.

Yield Curve
The relationship between interest rates and bond maturities. A flattening or inverted curve signals economic slowdown — a critical input for fixed income PMs. AI tools monitoring yield curve shifts in real time are among the highest-demand products in fixed income today.
Market Capitalisation
Price × shares outstanding. Large-cap (>$10bn), mid-cap ($2–10bn), small-cap (<$2bn). AI tools often need market cap filters built into their logic — analysts screen by cap tier before anything else.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E)
Share price ÷ earnings per share. The most-used valuation multiple. High P/E = growth expectations; low P/E = value or risk signal. AI parsing earnings filings must handle P/E comparisons carefully — norms vary sharply by sector.
Liquidity
How quickly an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Illiquid assets (some bonds, private equity) are harder to model with AI. Your product's data coverage may be constrained by asset liquidity — a key design decision.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net profit ÷ total shares. The fundamental unit of corporate profitability analysts track each quarter. AI parsing earnings calls must extract EPS accurately — small errors propagate through valuation models and erode trust fast.
Passive vs. Active Investing
Passive funds track an index (e.g. S&P 500); active managers pick stocks seeking alpha. The biggest structural shift in asset management in 30 years. AI tools serving active and passive teams need very different feature sets.

EU AI Act High-Risk Deadline Passed — Agentic Finance Applications Now Formally in Scope

August 2026 marked the EU AI Act's enforcement deadline for high-risk AI systems. Credit scoring, fraud detection, and automated financial decision-making are explicitly classified as high-risk. Financial institutions building agentic workflows are now required to maintain conformity assessments, audit logs, and human oversight mechanisms under binding law — not just best practice.

Why It Matters

No new FASB standard for AI disclosure as of mid-2026. The SEC is pointing to its existing materiality-based disclosure framework rather than writing new rules. Why It Matters

Judgment Is the Last PM Skill AI Cannot Replace — And the Most Underinvested

Product Leadership's 2026 analysis finds AI is systematically stripping performative PM work — market research synthesis, requirements documentation, roadmap formatting. What remains: judgment, strategy, and customer empathy. 59% of product professionals identify strategy and business acumen as the most important skills for the next three years. Prompt design is now table stakes, not a differentiator.

Why It Matters

Stop optimising for output quantity. The risk in an AI-fluent PM market is becoming a requirements-passer rather than a strategist. Your strongest differentiator is reading a room, making a call under ambiguity, and owning the outcome — not the tool you use to draft the spec.

  • Write a one-paragraph product strategy for your current product — not a feature, a strategic bet with a named trade-off

The AI PM Who Can Design Evaluations Is in a Different Tier

The Institute of AI PM's September 2026 knowledge hub identifies a decisive market split: AI PMs who can co-design model evaluation frameworks are commanding significantly higher compensation and influence than those who only write prompt requirements. The expectation has shifted from "be technical enough to talk to engineers" to "define what good looks like — and how you'll know when AI fails."

Why It Matters

Evaluation design — defining what a good AI output looks like before it ships — is the highest-leverage PM skill in this space. Without it, your product ships hallucinations and you only discover them when an analyst makes a bad call in a live market.

Research & Think About
  • Research LLM evaluation frameworks: RAGAS, HELM, and domain-specific finance evals — pick one and understand it properly
  • Write an evaluation rubric for one key user interaction in your product: what does "good" look like, and what does "failing" look like?
  • Ask your data science team: "What are you evaluating for right now?" Listen for what they wish a PM had defined for them

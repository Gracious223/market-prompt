Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's stories cover ETFs, equity exposure, and volatility-linked decision-making. Here are six terms that come up in conversations with portfolio managers and analysts — all distinct from last week's vocabulary.

Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio or asset's value over a given period. A 20% drawdown means the portfolio fell 20% from its highest point. AI tools are often evaluated on their ability to reduce max drawdown, not just increase returns.
EPS (Earnings Per Share)
A company's net profit divided by its number of shares outstanding. One of the most watched metrics in equity research. "Earnings season" — when companies report EPS — is a high-signal period your analysts spend significant time preparing for.
Short Selling
Borrowing a share, selling it, and hoping to buy it back cheaper later. A short seller profits when a stock falls. AI-powered short-selling signals are a growth area in quant hedge funds — and a compliance-sensitive topic at regulated firms.
ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)
A fund that tracks an index, sector, or strategy and trades on an exchange like a stock. Liquid, low-cost, and increasingly AI-managed — HIAI being today's live example. ETF product design is now a battleground for AI differentiation.
VIX (Volatility Index)
Often called the "fear gauge" — measures expected S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. High VIX signals uncertainty; low VIX signals calm. Your analysts monitor it as a regime indicator; AI products must know which market regime they're operating in.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
The per-share value of a fund's assets minus liabilities. For ETFs, NAV is calculated daily. Differences between a fund's NAV and its market price create arbitrage opportunities — relevant context for understanding how AI-managed funds like HIAI are priced and traded.

FCA · Good Practice

FCA Embeds Mills Review: Agentic AI Needs Continuous Assurance, Not Periodic Review

On October 19 the FCA published an updated AI good and poor practice framework embedding six of the seven Mills Review recommendations. The key addition: agentic AI systems must be subject to continuous assurance — not just periodic review. No new rules were created; existing Consumer Duty and SM&CR obligations apply.

Why It Matters

"Continuous assurance" is a product requirement, not a compliance checkbox. If you're building an agent that runs on a schedule, you need a monitoring layer that proves it behaved correctly on every run. Design for this from day one.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's October 19 good practice publication directly — study the agentic AI section
  • Research what "continuous assurance" looks like operationally — alerting, logging, model drift detection, output sampling
  • Map your current product's monitoring capability against the FCA's three themes: consumer outcomes, governance, operational resilience

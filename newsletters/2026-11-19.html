Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

These are six terms that come up constantly in the buy-side environment you're building for — and that come up differently now that AI is reshaping how they work in practice.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × total shares outstanding. It's how markets size a company. "Large-cap" (>$10B), "mid-cap" ($2–10B), "small-cap" (<$2B). AI has turbocharged large-cap concentration — the top 10 US stocks are ~37% of the S&P 500.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
Total assets minus liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. The "true" per-share value of a fund. For ETFs, if NAV diverges from market price, arbitrage mechanisms close the gap — a process AI is now accelerating.
Sharpe Ratio
Return per unit of risk taken (excess return ÷ standard deviation). The standard measure of risk-adjusted performance. A Sharpe above 1.0 is good; above 2.0 is exceptional. AI-driven strategies are being evaluated on this metric.
Beta
How much a stock moves relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with the market. Beta of 1.5 = 50% more volatile. AI-related stocks currently carry high beta — explaining the sharp selloff in the MSCI AI index this quarter.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares, selling them, and buying back later at a lower price to profit from a price fall. AI is both a target for short sellers and a tool for them — LLMs can now surface negative signals faster than humans.
Dark Pool
Private exchanges where large institutional trades are executed away from public markets to avoid moving prices. AI is used to route orders optimally across lit and dark venues — a critical part of execution desks your users work alongside.

94% of UK Financial Firms Have Firmwide AI Policies — Bank Explores Kill Switches for Agentic Trading

The fourth joint Bank of England and FCA AI survey (published 26 October) found 94% of UK financial firms now have firmwide AI policies and 84% have centralised AI governance fora. Why It Matters

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your analysts reach for every day — and what they mean for the AI products you build alongside them.

Sharpe Ratio
Return earned per unit of risk taken — calculated as excess return divided by standard deviation. Portfolio managers use it to compare strategies on a risk-adjusted basis. AI tools that surface Sharpe ratios across a portfolio are among the most widely adopted risk analytics features.
Yield Curve
A plot of bond yields across different maturities — typically 2-year vs. 10-year. An inverted yield curve (short rates above long rates) is a classic recession signal. Macro analysts watch it obsessively; AI tools that flag yield curve shifts in real time are high-priority buy-side requests.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
The per-share value of a fund's assets minus liabilities. For mutual funds and ETFs, NAV is calculated daily. For alternatives and private funds, NAV may only update quarterly — a data freshness problem that AI tools in private markets are actively trying to solve.
Short Selling
Borrowing and selling a security you don't own, expecting its price to fall, then buying it back cheaper. Hedge funds use short positions to hedge exposure or express negative views. High short interest in a stock is a signal analysts monitor — and a data feed AI tools can monitor continuously.
Market Capitalisation
Share price multiplied by shares outstanding — the total market value of a company. Market cap defines the investment universe: large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap. Many mandates restrict portfolio managers to specific market cap bands, which shapes the search space any AI stock-screening tool must respect.
Leverage
Using borrowed capital to amplify investment exposure. A fund with 2x leverage controls twice the assets its equity would otherwise allow. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses. AI risk tools that model portfolio leverage and stress-test it under adverse scenarios address one of the most critical buy-side risk management needs.

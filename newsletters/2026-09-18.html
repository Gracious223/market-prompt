Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

The vocabulary your portfolio managers and analysts use daily — mastering it lets you ask sharper questions and frame your product decisions in terms they recognise.

Short Selling
Borrowing and selling an asset you don't own, betting the price will fall so you can buy it back cheaper. Short sellers generate signals AI tools can surface — short interest data is a key alternative dataset for analysts.
Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01%. The unit analysts and PMs use for yields, spreads, and fee changes. "The spread tightened 25bps" means it fell by 0.25%. You'll hear this constantly — get fluent with it quickly.
Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding = total market value of a company. Used to classify companies: large-cap (>$10B), mid-cap, small-cap. Different AI products are often calibrated differently across these segments.
Sharpe Ratio
Return per unit of risk (standard deviation). A Sharpe of 1.0 is decent; above 2.0 is excellent. Portfolio managers optimise for it. AI tools that help improve Sharpe without adding risk are extremely valuable to your users.
Dark Pools
Private trading venues where large institutional orders execute away from public exchanges. They exist to reduce market impact. AI is used to optimise execution routing across lit and dark venues — a key area of buy-side AI investment.
Repo Market
Short-term borrowing where firms sell securities and agree to repurchase them. It's the plumbing of financial markets — how banks and funds manage overnight liquidity. AI monitoring of repo market stress is an emerging risk use case.

SEC · AI Washing

SEC Is Actively Hunting AI Washing — 2026 Exam Priorities Target False AI Claims by Investment Advisers

The SEC's 2026 examination priorities formally scrutinise firms that overstate AI capabilities in marketing, claim proprietary algorithms that are standard third-party tools, or present back-tested AI results as live performance. Firms must now produce written AI governance policies, algorithm due diligence documentation, and evidence of human oversight on material decisions.

Why It Matters

Every product claim you write — about personalisation, prediction, or insight generation — is now a regulatory risk vector in the US. Your PRD needs to distinguish between what AI does, what it assists, and what a human decides. Ambiguity is liability.

FCA · UK Posture

FCA Holds Firm on Principles-Based AI Regulation — But Policy Focus Shifts to Scale

The FCA continues to rely on existing frameworks — Consumer Duty, SM&CR, and operational resilience — rather than AI-specific rules. But more than 80% of UK financial services firms are now using or adopting AI, and the policy debate is shifting from adoption to large-scale deployment risk and systemic concentration.

Why It Matters

The FCA's lack of AI-specific rules is not a green light — it means the obligation to interpret existing rules for AI contexts falls on your firm, not the regulator. Your legal and compliance team owns that translation; your PRD should enable it.

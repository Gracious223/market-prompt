Lead Story · Cambridge Judge

Cambridge Judge's 2026 Global AI Report: 81% Adoption, 40% Profitability — The Execution Gap Is the Story

The Centre for Alternative Finance at Cambridge Judge Business School surveyed financial services firms globally. 81% are adopting AI at some level; 40% of those report advanced adoption. But only 40% report increased profitability. 55% of firms — and 63% of regulators — say they cannot measure the value of AI deployment. Fintechs lead incumbents; incumbents lead regulators. The dominant risks flagged: model hallucinations, opacity, and lack of explainability.

Why It Matters

Every product you build will hit this wall. If 55% of financial firms can't measure their AI's value, your product needs a measurability layer built in from day one — not retrofitted. Discovery question: how will your users demonstrate ROI to their CFO?

Research & Think About

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your analysts use daily — and what they mean for the AI products you build for them.

Earnings Per Share (EPS)
A company's net profit divided by its shares outstanding. EPS surprises — actual vs. analyst consensus — move stock prices. AI tools that parse earnings transcripts and compare them against consensus are among the most actively adopted by equity analysts.
Price-to-Earnings Ratio (P/E)
Share price divided by EPS — the most widely quoted valuation multiple. A high P/E signals growth expectations; a low P/E can mean value or distress. AI-driven screening tools rank stocks by P/E relative to sector peers in seconds.
Beta
A measure of a stock's volatility relative to the market. Beta > 1 means the stock amplifies market moves. Portfolio managers use beta to calibrate risk — your AI product's risk attribution outputs need to speak this language fluently.
Sector Rotation
The shift of investment capital between sectors (e.g. from tech to energy) as the economic cycle evolves. Buy-side analysts monitor rotation signals in real time — a key use case for AI tools that aggregate fund flow and positioning data.
Duration
A fixed income measure of interest rate sensitivity — how much a bond's price moves when rates change. A portfolio with high duration is more exposed to rate rises. Fixed income PMs manage duration actively; AI tools that model rate scenarios must handle it precisely.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a given period. Maximum drawdown is a standard risk metric. Portfolio managers care about drawdown as much as returns — an AI product that ignores downside risk framing will struggle to earn trust.

Lloyds Deploys Agentic AI Across 21 Million Accounts — First UK Bank to Go Live at Scale

Lloyds Banking Group has deployed an agentic AI framework across its 21 million customer accounts, making it the first UK bank to take agentic AI into live consumer banking. Multiple AI agents work in real time for fraud protection — surfacing and processing signals faster than human teams can. A full rollout of its AI-powered financial assistant is also under way across mortgages, vehicle finance, and insurance. Lloyds is recruiting 300 agentic AI specialists and targeting over £100 million in AI value in 2026, up from £50 million delivered in 2025.

Why It Matters

Lloyds isn't piloting — it's in production at scale. The benchmark for "agentic AI in UK financial services" now has a live reference point, and your internal stakeholders will use it. Understanding how Lloyds built its human-in-the-loop controls for regulated outputs is essential context for your product architecture.

Research & Think About
  • Read Lloyds' press release on its agentic AI fraud deployment — study the architecture description carefully: what is the human oversight layer, and why does it matter in a regulated context?
  • Understand what "multi-agent system" means in practice — how do specialist agents divide responsibility, and how is conflict between agents resolved?
  • Map the Lloyds use case to your own product: is your AI doing a single task or coordinating across multiple steps? If the latter, you're building an agentic system — and it needs different governance thinking.

FCA Consumer Duty Consultation Closes — Final Rules Expected Q1 2027

The FCA's Consumer Duty scope and proportionality consultation (CP26/23) closed on 18 September. It proposes to clarify when the Duty applies, support proportionate implementation for wholesale firms, and reduce unnecessary complexity in distribution chains. Final rules are expected in Q1 2027.

Why It Matters

Consumer Duty is the primary lens through which your AI product will be scrutinised. The clarified scope narrows your uncertainty window — Q1 2027 final rules give you a firm deadline to ensure your product's evidence trail is ready for examination.

Research & Think About

Building Agentic Products Requires Systems Design Thinking — Not Just Feature Thinking

As agentic AI reaches production in financial services, the PM role is shifting from feature-level decisions to systems-level design: defining how agents divide responsibility, where human checkpoints sit, and how failure states are handled. PMs who can think in terms of agent orchestration — not just user stories — are commanding a hiring premium in 2026.

Why It Matters

Lloyds' agentic fraud system and BCG's FP&A automation case both succeeded because someone designed the system — not just the feature. Your next product spec should describe agent responsibilities, handoffs, and oversight layers, not just screens and buttons.

Research & Think About

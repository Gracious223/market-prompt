Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

As an AI PM building tools for analysts and portfolio managers, you will encounter these six concepts daily. Each carries a specific meaning in institutional finance that differs from casual usage — knowing them precisely earns trust in the room.

Duration
A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes. A 5-year duration means the bond's price falls ~5% if rates rise 1%. AI tools that help analysts model duration risk across fixed-income portfolios are high-value.
Credit Default Swap (CDS)
Insurance against a borrower defaulting. If a firm's CDS spread widens, the market is pricing in more default risk. Analysts use CDS spreads as real-time signals for credit quality — a key data input for your product.
Options (Calls & Puts)
A call option gives the right to buy an asset at a set price; a put gives the right to sell. Options markets reveal market expectations about volatility and direction — data that AI agents can synthesise faster than any analyst manually.
Carry Trade
Borrowing in a low-interest-rate currency and investing in a high-rate one. Carry trades unwind rapidly in stress — a known source of cross-asset volatility. Macro analysts track carry exposure as a systemic risk signal.
VIX (Volatility Index)
The "fear gauge" — measures the market's expected 30-day volatility from S&P 500 options prices. A spike in VIX signals market stress. Portfolio managers watch it constantly; it's a useful real-time input for any market intelligence tool.
Enterprise Value (EV)
Total firm value: market cap + net debt. Unlike market cap alone, EV accounts for a company's debt load, making it the standard valuation basis for analysts comparing firms across capital structures — a core financial modelling concept.

FCA CEO Rathi: SM&CR Accountability Gap for AI Is Now Regulators' Sharpest Focus

In a landmark June 24 speech at techUK's AI in Financial Services conference, FCA CEO Nikhil Rathi said "legislation will never keep up" with AI development — but identified three sharper supervisory priorities: algorithmic collusion and herding in markets, SM&CR gaps where no named individual has documented accountability for each AI system, and AI-enabled market concentration risk.

Why It Matters

The SM&CR accountability gap has immediate product design implications. Any AI tool you ship to a regulated firm must support documented human oversight — who approved what output, on what basis. Without that, compliance teams will block deployment regardless of the alpha generation story.

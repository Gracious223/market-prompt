Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily — and that show up differently now that AI is reshaping how markets work in practice.

Basis Points (bps)
One hundredth of one percent. Used everywhere in fixed income and fees. A fund charging 50bps charges 0.50% annually. When analysts say "spreads widened 25bps," yields rose 0.25%. You'll hear this term every single day.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline of a portfolio or strategy. A 20% drawdown means the portfolio fell 20% from its high. Maximum drawdown is a standard risk metric — and one AI strategies are routinely benchmarked against.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes. A bond with 7-year duration falls ~7% if rates rise 1%. Critical for portfolio managers managing fixed income risk — and for AI models assessing rate sensitivity across a portfolio.
P/E Ratio
Share price divided by earnings per share. The most common equity valuation metric. High P/E signals high growth expectations. AI-related stocks currently trade at elevated P/Es — a key driver of the selloff risk Nomura flagged today.
Carry Trade
Borrowing in a low-rate currency and investing in a higher-yielding one to earn the rate differential. Popular cross-asset strategy — and a source of sudden market volatility when it unwinds quickly at scale.
Market Maker
A firm that continuously quotes buy and sell prices for a security, providing liquidity. Profits from the spread between bid and ask. AI is now central to how market makers price risk in real time — the FCA's trading venues review addressed exactly this.

Singapore's MAS Opens Agentic Money Sandbox — The First Regulated Space for AI-Initiated Financial Transactions

The Monetary Authority of Singapore, with the Future of Finance Institute, launched an Agentic Money Sandbox — a regulated safe space for firms to test financial transactions initiated by AI agents before seeking full authorisation. It covers autonomous payment and settlement processes. It is the most deliberate regulatory infrastructure for agentic finance built to date, and sets the template other regulators are now watching closely.

Why It Matters

The sandbox model is how regulators are approaching agentic AI everywhere — contained experimentation before broad permission. If your roadmap includes any autonomous agent workflow that touches transaction or settlement data, understanding this framework is essential product context.

Research & Think About
  • Read the MAS sandbox guidelines — what is testable and what stays off-limits under the current scope
  • Understand the difference between "supervised" and "autonomous" agent architectures in regulated financial contexts
  • Ask your compliance team: what would an internal agentic sandbox look like at your firm before you ship an agent to production?

FCA · UK

FCA Publishes First AI Good-Practice Findings — Audit Trails and Board Accountability Are the Gaps

The FCA's 19 October AI good-practice report is circulating widely across UK financial services this week. No new rules — but the FCA identified specific gaps: audit trails for AI decisions, board-level ownership of AI risk, and bias testing before deployment. Firms that can't demonstrate these practices are already exposed under Consumer Duty and SM&CR.

Why It Matters

The FCA isn't writing new rules — it's telling you what good looks like, and enforcement follows. Audit trails and board accountability aren't engineering features; they're product requirements you need to specify in every PRD for UK-regulated users.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's good-practice findings directly at fca.org.uk — one of the clearest AI governance documents published by any major financial regulator
  • Define what an "audit trail" looks like in your product — can a compliance officer recreate every AI-assisted decision from stored logs?
  • Under SM&CR, who at your firm is personally accountable when an AI output causes a poor customer outcome?

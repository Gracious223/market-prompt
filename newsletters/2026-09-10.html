Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six fresh terms today — all different from previous editions. These surface constantly in conversations between portfolio managers, traders, and analysts. Knowing them lets you ask better product questions and earn the room.

Beta
A stock's sensitivity to market movements. Beta of 1.0 = moves with the market; 1.5 = 50% more volatile. Portfolio managers target specific beta exposures. AI tools that dynamically monitor beta drift across a portfolio are increasingly standard.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
Total assets minus liabilities, per share. The "true" value of a fund at a point in time. Private equity and hedge funds report NAV periodically — analysts building valuation models always anchor to it.
Basis Points (bps)
One basis point = 0.01%. Used constantly in fixed income and rate discussions. "The Fed raised rates 25 bps" = 0.25%. AI tools summarising central bank communications need to parse bps precisely — small errors matter enormously.
Duration
A bond's price sensitivity to interest rate changes. A 10-year duration means a 1% rate rise causes a ~10% price fall. Fixed income analysts rely on duration models daily. AI systems handling bond portfolios must handle this correctly or cause real losses.
Dark Pools
Private exchanges where large institutional trades execute off the public order book to avoid moving prices. Quant funds use AI to detect dark pool activity as a signal. Regulators scrutinise them for transparency and fairness.
Short Selling
Borrowing a stock and selling it, betting the price will fall so you can buy it back cheaper. A key mechanism for price discovery. AI-powered short-interest analysis and sentiment tools are a growing product category in your space.

SEC Brings 6 AI Washing Cases in 2026 — $44M+ in Alleged Fraud, Pace Doubling

The SEC's AI washing enforcement campaign is escalating: six cases brought so far in 2026, with AI-related securities cases on pace to double year-on-year. The CETU enforcement unit, created February 2025, is the operational arm. The charge: overstating, misstating, or failing to substantiate AI capabilities — prosecuted under the Marketing Rule and Investment Advisers Act antifraud provisions.

Why It Matters

Every word in your product's marketing copy, every claim in your business case, is a potential disclosure liability. Precision about what your AI actually does — not what it aspires to do — is now a legal requirement.

FCA · UK

FCA's "Rethinking Regulation for the Age of AI" — Principles-Based, But Watching Closely

The FCA has confirmed its stance: no AI-specific rules, relying instead on Consumer Duty, SM&CR, and operational resilience frameworks. The Mills Review into how AI reshapes retail financial services is underway. The regulator commits to intervening only in "egregious failures" — but has broadened what counts as a failure to include unexplainable algorithmic outputs and unfair personalisation.

Why It Matters

"Principles-based" sounds permissive. It is not — it means the FCA can apply existing frameworks to AI outputs in ways that weren't anticipated. Explainability and audit trails are non-negotiable in your product design.

Research & Think About

  • What does a Consumer Duty "good outcome" look like for an AI-generated analyst recommendation?

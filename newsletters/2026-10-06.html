Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily — vocabulary that lets you hold your own in rooms where investment decisions are made and evaluated.

Sharpe Ratio
Return earned above the risk-free rate per unit of volatility. A Sharpe of 2.0 is exceptional; below 1.0 is weak. AI tools that claim to improve returns must also show Sharpe improvement — raw return without risk context is meaningless.
Yield Curve
A graph plotting bond yields across maturities (2-year, 10-year, 30-year). An "inverted" curve — where short-term yields exceed long-term — has historically preceded recessions. Macro-aware AI tools must monitor curve shape in real time.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell them now, expecting to buy them back cheaper later and pocket the difference. A core tool for hedge funds expressing negative views. AI sentiment and alternative data are increasingly used to identify short candidates.
Market Capitalisation
Share price × total shares outstanding. Classifies stocks as large-cap (>$10B), mid-cap ($2B–$10B), or small-cap (<$2B). Different caps carry different liquidity, volatility, and analyst coverage — all material for AI tool design.
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between the highest price a buyer will pay (bid) and the lowest a seller will accept (ask). A wide spread signals low liquidity. AI execution tools must factor spread into cost estimates when modelling trade impact.
Drawdown
Peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value over a given period. Maximum drawdown is a key risk metric. Portfolio managers use AI to model drawdown scenarios under stress — it's often a more honest measure of risk than standard deviation.

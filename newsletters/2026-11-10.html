Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily — the vocabulary you need to design products that earn their trust and survive scrutiny in investment team meetings.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × total shares outstanding. It classifies companies into large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap. AI tools that screen by market cap help analysts filter the investable universe quickly.
P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
Share price divided by earnings per share. A quick gauge of how expensively the market values a company's profits. AI valuations are notoriously hard to assess using traditional P/E — a live debate in 2026.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value over a period. Maximum drawdown is a key risk metric. Any AI strategy claiming strong returns needs its drawdown profile interrogated alongside the gains.
Sharpe Ratio
Risk-adjusted return: how much return a strategy earns per unit of risk taken. A higher Sharpe is better. FINQ's AI ETF performance should be evaluated on Sharpe, not raw return alone.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Highly liquid assets (large-cap equities) are cheaper to trade. AI-managed funds that hold illiquid assets face significant operational risk.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net profit divided by shares outstanding — a core measure of company profitability. AI tools that parse earnings releases to extract EPS surprises are among the most adopted research tools in 2026.

70% Deploy AI in the Front Office — Only 5% Call It "Fully Integrated"

SimCorp's InvestOps 2026 report found 70% of investment managers now deploy AI in the front office, up from roughly 10% a year ago. But a separate Acuity Analytics survey of 80 senior asset management figures found only 5% describe AI as fully integrated. The gap between deployment and integration is the product opportunity of 2026.

Why It Matters

Your users have AI running — but it isn't embedded in how they actually work. The adoption-to-integration gap is precisely where internal AI product teams operate and where the highest-value products are built.

Research & Think About

  • Define "fully integrated" for your own product — what would it look like in an analyst's daily workflow?
  • Interview one internal analyst: at what point in their day does AI disappear from their process?

FCA · UK · Published 19 Oct 2026

FCA Publishes AI Good Practice Guidance — Asset Manager Governance Is Under Scrutiny

The FCA issued its "AI in Financial Services: Good Practice and Areas for Improvement" paper on 19 October, targeting asset managers directly. It introduces no new rules — but documents governance failures: firms retrofitting controls after deployment, missing AI model inventories, and inadequate third-party oversight. Senior managers remain accountable even without being AI specialists.

Why It Matters

The FCA's examples of "poor practice" are a product checklist in reverse. Retrofitted governance, missing model inventories, and absent escalation triggers are exactly the gaps your product must not create for your firm.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA paper in full — available via fca.org.uk; map each poor-practice example to a risk in your own product
  • Understand what "AI model inventory" means operationally: what fields would yours contain?
  • Under the Senior Managers Regime: who at your firm is ultimately accountable for AI outputs in analyst tools?

In a widely-read November 2026 essay, PM writer Ant Murphy argues that AI is automating the delivery layer of product management — writing specs, triaging bugs, running user research synthesis — while the premium on strategic judgment, stakeholder leadership, and product vision is growing. Why It Matters

Research & Think About

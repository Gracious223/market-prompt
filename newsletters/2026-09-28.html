Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Portfolio managers and analysts use these six terms daily to measure performance, manage risk, and communicate decisions. Building this vocabulary is how you earn the room in product reviews and stakeholder conversations.

Drawdown
Maximum peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value before recovery. A 20% drawdown on a $1B fund means $200M lost at the worst point. AI stress-testing tools that model drawdown under tail scenarios are high-value for risk desks.
Sharpe Ratio
Measures risk-adjusted return: excess return divided by volatility. A higher Sharpe means more return per unit of risk. AI trade ideas must be evaluated on Sharpe, not raw return — otherwise you're ignoring the risk your analyst is taking on.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
The per-share value of a fund, calculated as total assets minus liabilities. For mutual funds and ETFs, NAV is the baseline pricing mechanism. Discrepancies between NAV and market price signal arbitrage opportunities.
Benchmark
A reference index (e.g. S&P 500, MSCI World) against which a portfolio's performance is measured. Portfolio managers are judged relative to their benchmark — AI products that help PMs understand outperformance sources are core to the workflow.
Liquidity
How quickly and cheaply an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Equities are highly liquid; private credit is not. Liquidity profiling is a key AI capability in portfolio construction and risk management.
Beta
An asset's sensitivity to market movements. Beta of 1.2 means the asset moves 20% more than the market. Low-beta portfolios are defensive; high-beta portfolios amplify market moves. AI factor models use beta extensively in portfolio construction.

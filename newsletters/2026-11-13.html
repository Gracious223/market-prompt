Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

These six concepts come up constantly in portfolio manager and analyst conversations. Build fluency here and you will follow their reasoning — and earn the room faster.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding. Large-cap (>$10B), mid-cap ($2–10B), small-cap (<$2B). AI tools often need to be calibrated differently across these tiers — data quality and coverage drop sharply in small-cap.
P/E Ratio
Price divided by earnings per share. A P/E of 25 means investors pay $25 for every $1 of profit. It is the most cited valuation metric in equities — high P/E implies high growth expectations, low P/E implies value or risk concerns.
Short Selling
Borrowing a stock and selling it, expecting the price to fall, then buying it back cheaper. Short sellers often do intensive fundamental research — AI tools that surface negative signals have direct relevance to this strategy.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline of a portfolio or strategy. A 20% drawdown means a fund fell 20% from its high point. Risk-conscious portfolio managers monitor maximum drawdown closely — it is a core metric for AI-generated strategy outputs to report.
Tracking Error
How much a portfolio's returns deviate from its benchmark. A low tracking error means the fund closely mirrors the index; a high one signals active deviation. AI tools that recommend overweights must surface the tracking-error impact.
Credit Spread
The yield gap between a corporate bond and a risk-free government bond of the same maturity. Widening spreads signal rising default risk or market stress — a key signal for fixed-income analysts that AI monitoring tools should track in real time.

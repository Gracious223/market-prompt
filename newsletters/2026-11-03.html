Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Today's hedge fund stories surface vocabulary that every analyst and portfolio manager uses daily. These six terms will help you speak credibly about market structure, valuation, and risk — the language your users live in.

P/E Ratio
Price-to-Earnings ratio: share price divided by earnings per share. A high P/E implies investors expect future growth; a low P/E can signal undervaluation or distress. AI company P/E ratios have been under scrutiny as earnings growth struggles to match infrastructure spend.
Beta
Measures a stock's volatility relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with the market. Beta > 1 = amplified swings. AI stocks have historically carried high beta — today's selloff reflects exactly this dynamic playing out across a crowded trade.
Yield Curve
A graph showing interest rates across bond maturities (e.g. 2-year vs. 10-year). An inverted yield curve (short rates above long) typically signals recession fears. It matters for AI valuations because high rates compress the present value of future earnings.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of assets a fund or firm manages. A £10B AUM hedge fund and a £100M one have very different capacity constraints and execution pressures. AI products built for institutional users must account for scale of position.
Long/Short Equity
A hedge fund strategy: buy stocks expected to rise (long) and short stocks expected to fall. De-grossing — as Morgan Stanley describes — means closing both sides of the book simultaneously, which amplifies selling pressure across the market.
Risk-Adjusted Return
Return evaluated against the risk taken to achieve it. The Sharpe ratio is the most common metric: return above the risk-free rate, divided by volatility. AI tools that help analysts find higher Sharpe strategies — not just higher returns — are genuinely valuable.

FCA Publishes First AI Good and Poor Practice Guidance for Financial Firms

The FCA published its AI good and poor practice paper on October 19, covering governance, accountability, consumer outcomes, and operational resilience for generative and agentic AI. Firms remain responsible for all AI outputs — built, bought, or deployed. Poor practice includes launching automated journeys without adequate testing and human oversight becoming box-ticking.

Why It Matters

This isn't a consultation — it's live regulatory expectation. Your PRD needs a section on accountability, testing, and ongoing monitoring that maps directly to this guidance. Building without it is a compliance risk, not just a product gap.

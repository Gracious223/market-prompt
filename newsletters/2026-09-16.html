Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your analysts use every day — building fluency here is how you stop nodding along in product reviews and start asking the right questions.

Market Capitalisation
Share price × shares outstanding. Used to classify stocks: large-cap (>£10B), mid-cap, small-cap. Determines index membership and position sizing constraints for fund managers.
Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
Share price ÷ earnings per share. High P/E implies the market expects future growth; low P/E may signal undervaluation or distress. One of the first multiples analysts reach for in valuation.
Beta
A stock's volatility relative to the market. Beta > 1 amplifies market moves; beta < 1 cushions them. Portfolio managers use it to calibrate risk exposure. AI tools that model beta in real time are high-value.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell now, intending to buy back cheaper later. A mechanism for profiting from falling prices and for price discovery. Regulators watch AI-assisted short strategies closely.
Earnings Per Share (EPS)
Net profit ÷ shares outstanding. The single most-watched metric in quarterly earnings. AI tools that accelerate EPS modelling and compare actuals vs. consensus estimates are among the highest-demand use cases.
Liquidity
How quickly an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Illiquid markets amplify losses in stress events. AI portfolio tools must account for liquidity constraints — ignoring them is a product failure.

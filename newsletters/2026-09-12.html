Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your analysts use daily — the vocabulary you need to hold your own in product conversations about what to surface, flag, and calculate for buy-side users.

Short Selling
Borrowing a stock and selling it, expecting to buy it back cheaper later. Profit if the price falls. AI tools that surface short interest data and borrow costs help analysts model bearish positioning and crowded trades.
Dividend Yield
Annual dividends paid ÷ current share price. High yield can signal income or distress — context matters. Income-focused PMs use dividend screens constantly; AI parsing dividend policy changes from earnings calls is high-value.
Beta
A measure of a stock's volatility relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with the market. Beta >1 = amplified swings. Risk management tools built for PMs must surface beta clearly — it drives portfolio hedging decisions.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio or asset over a period. Maximum drawdown is a key risk metric. PMs managing mandates with drawdown limits need real-time monitoring — a core AI product use case.
Sector Rotation
Shifting portfolio allocation between sectors (e.g. tech to energy) as the economic cycle turns. AI tools tracking macro signals to flag rotation opportunities are among the most actively sought by macro-aware buy-side teams.
Benchmark
An index (e.g. FTSE 100, S&P 500, MSCI World) used to measure a portfolio's performance. Most PMs are benchmarked — every AI product output should know what benchmark the user is measured against before generating a recommendation.

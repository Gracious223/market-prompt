Finance Teams Speed Up Tasks with AI — But Still Can't Fix the Cash Cycle

A GlobeNewswire report finds finance teams are accelerating individual tasks with AI but failing to address the underlying structural problem — the cash conversion cycle. AI optimises inputs; it doesn't redesign broken process architecture. Speed is being added on top of dysfunction, not instead of it.

Why It Matters

Speed is a vanity metric. Automating an analyst's workflow without redesigning the underlying process produces faster wrong answers. Your product's real value proposition must be process transformation, not just task acceleration — and your discovery work needs to find where the structural problems live.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six more terms your analysts and portfolio managers use every day — and what they mean for the AI products you build alongside them.

Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell them, hoping to buy back cheaper later and pocket the difference. Short interest data is a key sentiment signal — AI tools that aggregate it help analysts identify crowded trades and potential short-squeeze risks before they move the market.
Credit Spread
The yield difference between a corporate bond and a risk-free government bond of the same maturity. Spreads widen in risk-off environments and tighten when sentiment improves. Fixed income PMs track spread compression and widening in real time as a proxy for market stress.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
The per-unit value of a fund: total assets minus liabilities, divided by units outstanding. Calculated daily for most funds. The data pipelines feeding NAV calculations are a significant AI integration point in fund operations — accuracy failures here have direct investor consequences.
Market Capitalisation
A company's total market value — share price multiplied by shares outstanding. Large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap are the first filters in equity screening. Every AI research tool your analysts use segments the investable universe by market cap as a baseline.
Yield Curve
A line plotting bond yields across different maturities — typically 2-year to 10-year. An inverted curve (short rates above long rates) has historically preceded recessions. Macro analysts and fixed income PMs watch it as closely as almost any other single indicator.
Liquidity
How quickly an asset can be bought or sold without significantly moving its price. A core constraint in portfolio construction. AI tools that generate trade recommendations must account for liquidity — a theoretically optimal trade is worthless if the market is too thin to execute it at scale.

Lead Story · BCG

One CFO Automated 90% of FP&A in 9 Months — But Most Firms Still Can't Bridge the Expectation-Impact Gap

BCG's 2026 research on CFOs scaling AI agents reveals a stark split: one early-mover CFO automated over 90% of financial planning and analysis work in nine months. Yet nearly 90% of CEOs who expect measurable AI ROI cannot point to consistent results. BCG's diagnosis is structural — the barrier is no longer model capability but data readiness, process design, and skill gaps. Firms that bridge it see 2.3x ROI within 13 months; those that don't are still running pilots.

Why It Matters

The CFO who automated 90% of FP&A almost certainly had clean, consistent data as a pre-condition. Your product will hit the same structural ceiling — data readiness is not an engineering problem you can route around; it's a discovery finding you need to surface before you write a line of product spec. The 90% result is a ceiling, not a default.

Research & Think About
  • Read BCG's "How CFOs Are Funding and Scaling AI Agents in 2026" — it's specific about where the value gap lives and what separates firms that cross it from those that don't
  • Understand FP&A as a workflow: why is financial planning and analysis more automatable today than equity research? What's structurally different about the data?
  • Ask your engineering team: what percentage of the data your AI product relies on is production-ready right now? That number is your adoption ceiling — and it belongs in your discovery findings.

FCA · UK

FCA Sets Out "Next Phase" of Smarter Regulation — Outcomes-Based Accountability Sharpens

The FCA has published its next phase of regulatory approach, doubling down on outcomes-focused, principles-based oversight. No new AI-specific rules — but significantly sharper accountability expectations. Firms must now demonstrate what consumers and markets actually experienced, not just what processes they followed.

Why It Matters

"No new rules" does not mean no accountability. The outcomes framing shifts regulatory scrutiny from "what did you build?" to "what did your users experience?" For an AI product in financial services, that means your evaluation framework is not optional — it's the evidence base your compliance team will need when the FCA comes knocking.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's "next phase of smarter, more effective regulation" statement — map each outcome category against what your AI product currently produces for users
  • Under Consumer Duty and SM&CR, define who at your firm is accountable when an AI-assisted recommendation leads to a poor client outcome
  • Build "outcomes evidence" into your product roadmap now: what data would you show the FCA to demonstrate your tool is producing good outcomes consistently?

Role Evolution · Ant Murphy

How Product Is Changing in 2026: Smaller Teams, Deeper Ownership, Less Room to Hide

Murphy's 2026 analysis finds AI is making product teams smaller and more specialised — each PM owns a narrower but deeper slice of the product. The shift isn't just about skills; it's about accountability. PMs who can direct AI agents, not just brief engineers, are the ones whose roles are expanding fastest. The generalist middle is hollowing out.

Why It Matters

"Narrower but deeper" is exactly the trajectory for an AI PM in financial services. The firms that benefit most from this shift are the ones where the PM genuinely understands the domain — which is your structural edge over any generalist AI PM. The risk: letting AI handle discovery busywork while quietly losing the judgment muscle that makes the role defensible.

AI Strips PM Work Down to Its Hardest Core: Judgment Is Now the Only Defensible Skill

2026 product management research (ProductLeadership, ProductSchool, Airtable) converges on a single finding: AI is automating the performative work of product management — research, documentation, analysis, reporting — leaving behind the hardest skill, which is judgment. 59% of product professionals cite strategy and business acumen as the most critical PM skill for the next three years, ranking above technical skills.

Why It Matters

If AI handles discovery busywork, the differentiator is the quality of your bets. Domain fluency in financial services AI — knowing why a portfolio manager would trust or distrust an AI output — is judgment that cannot be prompted. It is built through exposure. It is what your role is accumulating daily whether you track it or not.

Research & Think About
  • Read "How AI Is Changing Product Management in 2026" at productleadership.com — one of the more grounded analyses of where the PM skill premium actually sits right now
  • Audit your last three product decisions: how much was data-driven, and how much was judgment that data could not fully resolve? That ratio is your current PM signature.
  • Ask: what would a portfolio manager need to see before trusting an AI output enough to act on it? That's your product's trust architecture — and it starts with your judgment about what the model should and should not be asked to do.

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six terms that surface in analyst conversations daily — vocabulary that lets you work credibly alongside portfolio managers and understand the market context your products operate in.

Short Selling
Borrowing a security and selling it, hoping to buy it back cheaper later. Short sellers profit from falling prices and are often first to signal stress in a stock or bond. AI-driven short-seller reports have moved markets in 2026.
Market Maker
A firm that continuously quotes buy and sell prices to provide liquidity. Without market makers, trading slows dramatically. AI is being used to price spreads and manage inventory in real time across major market-making desks.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of assets a fund or manager controls. A $10B fund has $10B AUM. AUM is the standard measure of a fund's size and directly drives fee revenue — critical context when building products for asset managers.
Benchmark
The reference index against which a portfolio's performance is measured — e.g. S&P 500 for US equities. Outperforming the benchmark is "generating alpha." AI tools must understand a user's benchmark to frame returns correctly.
Liquidity
How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving the price. High liquidity means large, quick trades at stable prices. CoreWeave's bond stress this week is partly a liquidity story — thin secondary markets amplify price moves.
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between the price a buyer offers (bid) and the price a seller asks. A wide spread signals low liquidity or high uncertainty. In distressed markets — like AI debt in October 2026 — spreads widen significantly, signalling stress.

FCA · Good Practice

FCA Publishes First AI Good Practice Standards — Three Themes, No New Rules

On 19 October the FCA published good and poor practice examples for AI in financial services — covering consumer outcomes, governance and accountability, and operational resilience. No new rules are created, but firms are expected to map existing obligations to AI use. The FCA will update examples as the technology evolves.

Why It Matters

"Good practice examples" function like informal guidance — your regulators are watching whether you follow them. Consumer outcomes, governance, and resilience are now your product's three non-negotiable pillars.

Research & Think About
  • Read the FCA's good practice page directly — study the vulnerability example (live flagging of at-risk customers via chatbot)
  • Map the three themes (consumer outcomes, governance, resilience) to a specific design decision in your current product
  • Research what "proportionate" AI governance means under existing FCA frameworks — how does firm size affect obligations?

AI PMs Must Master Evaluation — Outputs Shift, Hallucinate, and Behave Differently Across Users

A KDnuggets guide (October 20) draws a sharp line: traditional PM systems are deterministic; AI outputs vary, hallucinate, and behave differently across user populations. The emerging skill gap is knowing when not to trust an output and how to design evaluation systems that catch problems before users do.

Why It Matters

For AI tools used in investment decisions, a hallucinated output is a liability event. Designing eval frameworks into your product from day one is both risk mitigation and a competitive moat — most PMs aren't doing it.

Research & Think About
  • Research LLM evaluation frameworks (RAGAS, LangSmith, Braintrust) — understand what metrics matter for financial AI outputs
  • Read about "model calibration" — the difference between a confident wrong answer and a well-calibrated uncertain one
  • Design a simple eval rubric for one feature in your current product: what does "good output" actually look like?

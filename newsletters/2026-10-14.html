Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

This week's themes — AI debt, valuation risk, and trust gaps in autonomous finance — all live in a markets vocabulary your users use daily. Six terms you should know cold before your next conversation with a portfolio manager.

Dark Pool
A private trading venue where large institutional orders are matched away from public exchanges. Used to minimise market impact. AI is increasingly used to route orders intelligently across dark and lit venues.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a period. Maximum drawdown is a key risk metric. When the BoE warns of AI valuation corrections, drawdown risk is what portfolio managers are pricing.
Sharpe Ratio
Return per unit of risk: (portfolio return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation. A higher Sharpe means better risk-adjusted returns. AI systems are evaluated on whether they improve Sharpe, not just raw returns.
Duration
A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes — measured in years. A bond with 10-year duration falls ~10% when rates rise 1%. Critical context as AI-related debt issuance balloons into rate-sensitive markets.
Credit Spread
The yield difference between a corporate bond and a risk-free government bond of the same maturity. Wider spreads signal higher perceived default risk. Spread widening is an early warning sign of the correction the BoE is watching for.
Mark-to-Market
Valuing assets at current market prices, not historical cost. Relevant when AI investment portfolios hold illiquid assets — like private AI companies — where "mark-to-market" is a contested, model-driven exercise.

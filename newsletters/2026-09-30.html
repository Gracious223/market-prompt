Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Understanding how capital moves and how risk is priced is the foundation for building AI products your users will trust. These six concepts appear constantly in analyst and portfolio manager conversations.

Liquidity
How quickly and cheaply an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price. Analysts building with AI need to know which data is liquid (equities) vs. illiquid (private credit) — it changes what's architecturally possible.
Beta
A measure of how much an asset moves relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with the market; >1 = amplified moves. The counterpart to Alpha — managing beta is as important as seeking returns above it.
Benchmark
A reference index (e.g. S&P 500, MSCI World) against which performance is measured. Your AI tools are judged against the same logic — is the output better than the human baseline?
Systematic vs. Discretionary
Systematic funds use rules-based, model-driven strategies. Discretionary funds rely on human judgment. Most AI-augmented buy-side tools sit in the grey zone between these two investment styles.
Mark-to-Market (MTM)
Valuing an asset at its current market price rather than cost. MTM creates volatility in P&L statements. AI tools with real-time pricing must account for MTM fluctuations in their outputs.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
The total value of a fund's assets minus liabilities, divided by shares outstanding. NAV is how mutual funds and ETFs are priced. AI portfolio tools often track NAV drift to flag rebalancing needs.

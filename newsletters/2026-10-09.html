US Regulation · Model Risk

Fed SR 26-2: AI/ML Now Under Model Risk Rules — But Agentic AI Has No Oversight Framework Yet

The Federal Reserve's April 2026 SR 26-2 explicitly extends model risk management to AI and machine learning models, superseding SR 11-7. But in a notable carve-out, generative and agentic AI are described as "novel and rapidly evolving" — and excluded from scope pending a separate Request for Information. A Wolters Kluwer survey found 72% of banking professionals cite kill-switch protocols as their least-prepared area.

Why It Matters

The regulatory gap around agentic AI is a window of ambiguity that won't last. The RFI is coming, and firms that govern agentic systems today will write the de facto standard. Your audit logs and override mechanisms are being designed before the regulator asks for them — or after.

Research & Think About
  • Read SR 26-2's materiality framework — what makes a model "high risk" under the new guidance?
  • What is the difference between an AI model (covered by SR 26-2) and an AI agent (currently excluded)? Understand the line before it matters in a compliance review
  • Research what "kill-switch protocol" means in practice for a financial AI system — then ask if your product has one

Public Markets 101
Essential concepts for your role

Six terms your portfolio managers and analysts use daily — each one a building block for the conversations you'll have as you build AI products alongside them.

Beta
A measure of a security's volatility relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with the market; above 1 = amplified swings. AI risk tools model portfolio beta in real time to flag exposure during market stress.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
The per-share value of a fund's assets minus liabilities, calculated daily. NAV is the price at which fund shares are bought and sold. AI systems flag NAV anomalies or pricing errors at scale across large fund families.
Drawdown
The peak-to-trough decline in a portfolio's value over a given period. Maximum drawdown is a key risk metric. AI portfolio tools monitor drawdown thresholds and trigger rebalancing signals when limits are approached.
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total market value of assets a firm or fund manager is responsible for. AUM signals scale, revenue potential, and influence. AI product value is often measured by % of AUM it touches or informs.
Active vs. Passive Management
Active managers pick securities to beat a benchmark; passive funds track an index cheaply. AI is blurring the line — quantitative active strategies now run at near-passive cost but with active signal generation.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell them, betting the price will fall, then buying them back cheaper to profit. AI tools analyse short interest data as a contrarian sentiment signal — high short interest can flag hidden risk or mean-reversion opportunities.

Basel Committee · Global Banks

Basel Committee: AI and Cyber Risk Will Force a Rewrite of Operational Risk Categories

At its 28–29 September meeting, the Basel Committee agreed to review the operational risk framework's loss event-type categories with a specific focus on cyber and AI risk. The committee noted AI may support efficiency gains but could also amplify operational, cyber, and concentration risks, and said AI in critical financial functions requires careful governance and ongoing supervisory attention.

Why It Matters

When the Basel Committee rewrites loss categories, banks rewrite their risk frameworks — and your product's governance requirements move with them. Concentration risk from AI vendors and operational resilience of AI systems will become capital questions, not just operational ones.

Research & Think About
  • Read the BIS October 1 press release directly — note which AI risks they flagged as most pressing
  • Understand what "concentration risk" means when a firm depends on a single AI vendor for critical workflows — how would you mitigate this in your product design?
  • Research how operational risk capital is calculated under Basel III — why does the loss category taxonomy matter?

FCA · UK Parliament

UK Treasury Committee: FCA Must Publish Practical AI Guidance by End of 2026

Parliament's Treasury Committee has formally recommended the FCA publish "comprehensive, practical guidance" for firms on consumer protection and senior manager accountability for AI harms — with a year-end 2026 deadline. The FCA's current position remains that SM&CR and Consumer Duty govern AI outputs, with no AI-specific rules planned, but the second AI Live Testing cohort and the Mills Review are shaping what that guidance will contain.

Why It Matters

If you're building for UK-regulated users, the accountability regime for AI harms is being written right now. The guidance will determine which Senior Manager Function holder is liable when an AI-influenced investment decision goes wrong — that design choice belongs in your PRD.

Research & Think About
  • Read the Treasury Committee's recommendation in Slaughter and May's October 1 bulletin — understand what "comprehensive, practical guidance" means vs. the FCA's current principles-only stance
  • Under SM&CR, which SMF holder at your firm would be accountable for an AI output that caused customer harm?
  • Understand what the Mills Review is examining — how might its findings reshape the FCA's stance on AI in retail financial services?
